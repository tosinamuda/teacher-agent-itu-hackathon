*explanation*

## Objective lop02

### Candidate A
#### Finding the LCM of whole numbers

*explanation*

The **lowest common multiple (LCM)** of two or more whole numbers is the smallest number that is a multiple of each given number. A *multiple* is a number that can be divided by another number without a remainder (REF‑1). A *common multiple* is simply a multiple shared by the numbers involved (REF‑2).

*worked_example*

**Worked example (prime‑factor method):** Find the LCM of 12 and 18.

1. Prime‑factorise each number:
   - 12 = 2² × 3¹
   - 18 = 2¹ × 3²
2. For each prime, take the highest power that appears: 2² and 3².
3. Multiply these highest powers: 2² × 3² = 4 × 9 = **36**.
Thus, LCM(12, 18) = 36. This illustrates how the LCM is a common multiple (REF‑2).

#### Finding the LCM of whole numbers

*explanation*

The **lowest common multiple (LCM)** of two or more whole numbers is the smallest whole number that each given number divides exactly. A quick way to find it is to use prime‑factorisation: for each prime that appears, keep the highest power found in any of the numbers and multiply those together. This definition follows the textbook description (REF‑1).

*worked_example*

**Worked example (prime‑factor method):** Find the LCM of 12 and 18.

1. Prime‑factorise: 12 = 2² × 3¹, 18 = 2¹ × 3².
2. Take the highest power of each prime: 2² and 3².
3. Multiply: 2² × 3² = 4 × 9 = **36**.
Thus LCM(12, 18) = 36. The steps mirror the textbook example (REF‑2).
