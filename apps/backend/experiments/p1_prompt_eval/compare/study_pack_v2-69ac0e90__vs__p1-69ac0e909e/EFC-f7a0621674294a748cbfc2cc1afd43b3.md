### Candidate A
#### Ordering fractions – ascending order (challenge)

*explanation*

To compare fractions quickly, give them a common denominator. Once the denominators match, the size of each fraction is determined solely by its numerator; a larger numerator means a larger fraction. Finding the least common denominator (LCD) puts all fractions on the same scale before ordering them.

*explanation*

**Worked example**

Order \(\frac{3}{8}, \frac{1}{4}, \frac{5}{12}\) in ascending order.
1. LCD of 8, 4, 12 is 24.
2. Convert:
   - \(\frac{3}{8}=\frac{9}{24}\)
   - \(\frac{1}{4}=\frac{6}{24}\)
   - \(\frac{5}{12}=\frac{10}{24}\)
3. Compare numerators (6, 9, 10). Order: \(\frac{6}{24},\frac{9}{24},\frac{10}{24}\) → \(\frac{1}{4},\frac{3}{8},\frac{5}{12}\).

*explanation*

### Candidate B
#### Ordering fractions – ascending order

*explanation*

When fractions share the same denominator, their size is reflected directly by the numerator. By converting each fraction to a common (least common) denominator we can compare them easily and list them from smallest to largest. This idea is illustrated in the textbook where fractions such as \(\frac{5}{6}, \frac{7}{8}, \frac{3}{4}\) are arranged in ascending order.

_Source: REF‑1_

*worked_example*

**Worked example**

Order the fractions \(\frac{8}{9}, \frac{11}{12}, \frac{5}{6}\) in ascending order.

1. Identify the LCD of 9, 12, and 6 → LCD = 36.
2. Convert each fraction to the LCD:
   - \(\frac{8}{9} = \frac{8\times4}{9\times4} = \frac{32}{36}\)
   - \(\frac{11}{12} = \frac{11\times3}{12\times3} = \frac{33}{36}\)
   - \(\frac{5}{6} = \frac{5\times6}{6\times6} = \frac{30}{36}\)
3. Compare the numerators (30, 32, 33). Ordering from smallest to largest gives \(\frac{30}{36}, \frac{32}{36}, \frac{33}{36}\), which simplifies back to \(\frac{5}{6}, \frac{8}{9}, \frac{11}{12}\).

This follows the conversion process described in the textbook exercise.

_Source: REF‑2_

## Objective lop02

### Candidate A
#### Arrange fractions in descending order

*explanation*

When comparing fractions, we first rewrite them with a **common denominator**. Because equivalent fractions have the same value, putting the fractions over the same denominator lets us judge their size simply by looking at the numerators. Using the *least* common denominator keeps the numbers as small as possible, which makes the comparison quicker. (REF‑1, REF‑2)

*worked_example*

**Worked Example – Descending order**

Given: \(\frac{5}{8},\; \frac{8}{14},\; \frac{18}{28}\)

1. **Find the LCD** – the smallest number divisible by 8, 14 and 28 is 56.
2. **Convert** each fraction:
   - \(\frac{5}{8}=\frac{5\times7}{8\times7}=\frac{35}{56}\)
   - \(\frac{8}{14}=\frac{8\times4}{14\times4}=\frac{32}{56}\)
   - \(\frac{18}{28}=\frac{18\times2}{28\times2}=\frac{36}{56}\)
3. **Compare numerators** – 36 > 35 > 32, so \(\frac{18}{28} > \frac{5}{8} > \frac{8}{14}\).
(REF‑3)

*practice*

**Practice – Stretch problem**

Arrange the following fractions in **descending** order using the LCD method:
\[\frac{7}{12},\; \frac{5}{9},\; \frac{11}{15},\; \frac{3}{8}\]
1. Find the LCD of 12, 9, 15 and 8.
2. Rewrite each fraction with that denominator.
3. List the fractions from the largest numerator to the smallest.
(Recall that equivalent fractions keep the same value – REF‑1, and that the LCD gives a fair basis for comparison – REF‑2.)

### Candidate B
#### Arrange fractions in descending order (extended)

*explanation*

To order fractions from largest to smallest we rewrite each fraction with a common denominator.  Using the least common denominator (LCD) creates equivalent fractions that share the same denominator, so the comparison reduces to looking only at the numerators (REF‑1).

*worked_example*

**Worked example (from the textbook)**

*Step 1 – Find the LCD*\
The denominators 8, 14 and 28 have LCD = 56.

*Step 2 – Convert each fraction*\
- \(\frac{5}{8}=\frac{5\times7}{8\times7}=\frac{35}{56}\)\
- \(\frac{8}{14}=\frac{8\times4}{14\times4}=\frac{32}{56}\)\
- \(\frac{18}{28}=\frac{18\times2}{28\times2}=\frac{36}{56}\)

*Step 3 – Compare numerators*\
The numerators 36, 35, 32 give the descending order \(\frac{18}{28}>\frac{5}{8}>\frac{8}{14}\).
