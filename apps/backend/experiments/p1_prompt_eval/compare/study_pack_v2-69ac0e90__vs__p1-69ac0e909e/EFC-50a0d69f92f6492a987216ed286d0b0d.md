Remember the place‑value positions: the right‑most three zeros are the thousands, the next three are the millions.  This clear separation helps avoid mixing up 300 000 (three hundred thousand) with 3 000 000 (three million).

#### Write numbers in millions

*explanation*

When we write a number in millions we are describing how many groups of **1,000,000** are present. To change a mixed digit‑word expression such as 0.835 million into a standard numeral, move the decimal point six places to the right: 0.835 → 835,000. The same rule gives 20.05 million → 20,050,000.

**Why place‑value matters**: A common slip is to forget that each "million" equals six zeros. For example, 4,200,000 is **4.2 million**, not 42 million, because the digits "42" would represent 42 million (42,000,000). Keeping track of the six zeros helps avoid this error.

   • 9,750,000 ÷ 1,000,000 = 9.75.
   • Write the answer as **9.75 million**.

### Candidate B
#### Writing billions in digits and words

*explanation*

A **billion** is defined as **one thousand million**. Because a million is 1,000,000 (see REF‑1), a billion is 1,000 × 1,000,000 = 1,000,000,000.

When writing a number that contains billions, place the billions digit(s) to the left of the first comma that separates the millions. Then write the remaining millions, thousands, and units, each followed by a comma.

**Step‑by‑step process**
1. Identify how many billions are present (how many times 1,000,000,000 fits into the number).
2. Write that figure to the left of the first comma.
3. Identify the remaining millions, thousands and units and write them in the usual three‑digit groups separated by commas.
4. Finally, express the full name by naming each group: *billion*, *million*, *thousand*, and the units.

**Example:** 2 billion 350 million 120 thousand 5
- Billions part: 2 → write **2** left of the first comma.
- Millions part: 350 → write **350** after the first comma.
- Thousands part: 120 → write **120** after the second comma.
- Units part: 5 → write **5** after the third comma.

Resulting digits: **2,350,120,005**.

Resulting words: *two billion three hundred fifty million one hundred twenty thousand five*.

*Note:* Because a billion is simply a thousand millions, you can always check your answer by counting groups of three zeroes after the billions digit.

*worked_example*

**Worked example 1:** Convert **0.15 billion** to digits and write its full name.

1. Recognise that 0.15 billion means 0.15 × 1,000,000,000.
2. Multiply: 0.15 × 1,000,000,000 = 150,000,000.
3. Write the digits: **150,000,000**.
4. Because the result is less than one full billion, express the name in millions: **one hundred fifty million**.

Thus, 0.15 billion = 150,000,000 = *one hundred fifty million*.
