## Objective lop02

### Candidate A
#### Finding the LCM of whole numbers

*explanation*

The **lowest common multiple (LCM)** of two or more whole numbers is the smallest number that is a multiple of each of the given numbers.

*worked_example*

**Example (using prime factorisation):** Find the LCM of 12 and 18.

1. Prime factorise each number:
   - 12 = 2² × 3¹
   - 18 = 2¹ × 3²
2. For each prime, take the highest power that appears:
   - 2² (from 12) and 3² (from 18).
3. Multiply these highest powers: 2² × 3² = 4 × 9 = **36**.
Thus, LCM(12, 18) = 36. This follows the method described in the textbook.

### Candidate B
#### Finding the LCM of whole numbers

*explanation*

The **lowest common multiple (LCM)** of two or more whole numbers is the smallest number that is a multiple of each of the given numbers. A multiple is a number divisible by another without a remainder, and a common multiple is a number that works for both numbers (see REF‑1 and REF‑2).

*worked_example*

**Worked Example (prime factorisation):** Find the LCM of 12 and 18.
1. 12 = 2² × 3, 18 = 2 × 3²
2. Take the highest powers of each prime: 2² and 3²
3. Multiply them: 2² × 3² = 36.
Thus, LCM(12, 18) = 36.
