### Candidate A
#### Counting in Millions – Remedial Review

*explanation*

A **million** is written as **1,000,000**.  This means:
1. Write the digit **1**.
2. Follow it with **six zeros** – each zero represents a power of ten.
3. In scientific notation the same number is written $10^{6}$, because the digit 1 is multiplied by ten six times.

Seeing the six zeros can be tricky; a useful trick is to group them in sets of three: **1 000 000**.  The first group after the leading 1 is the *hundred‑thousands* place, the next is the *millions* place.  Keeping the groups together helps avoid the place‑value errors you encountered in the diagnostic questions.

*explanation*

To picture one million, think of staying awake for more than **11½ days** (about 1,000,000 seconds) or walking a distance of **1,000,000 kilometres** – roughly the distance from the Earth to the Moon and back three times.  When you count in millions, you move the whole group of six zeros together.  For example, after **2,000,000** comes **3,000,000**, not **20,000,000**.  This reinforces the idea that the *million* place moves as a single block.

A common slip is to place a zero in the wrong group (e.g., writing 300,000 for three million).  Remember: the digit representing *millions* is always followed by **six zeros**.

*worked_example*

**Worked Example – Writing five million one hundred thousand**

**Problem:** Write the number “five million one hundred thousand” in digits.

**Step‑by‑step solution:**
1. Identify the *million* part: “five million” → put the digit **5** in the millions place.
2. After the millions digit, write **six zeros** to fill the lower places: **5 000 000**.
3. Identify the *hundred‑thousand* part: “one hundred thousand” → add **1** in the hundred‑thousands place, which is the first zero after the millions digit.
4. Replace that first zero with **1** and keep the remaining five zeros: **5 100 000**.

**Answer:** **5,100,000**.

Notice how the digit for the hundred‑thousands sits directly after the millions digit; this prevents the place‑value confusion seen in the diagnostic items.

**Practice – Counting to three million**

Using the number chart provided in the figure, count aloud from **2,000,000** to **3,000,000** in steps of **one hundred‑thousand**.

- Start at **2,000,000**.
- Add **100,000** each time: 2,100,000 – 2,200,000 – … – 2,900,000 – 3,000,000.
- Say each number clearly, paying attention to the position of the six zeros.

**Hints:**
- Remember the pattern: the digit in the *million* place stays the same until you reach the next million.
- Keep the groups of three zeros together (e.g., 2 100 000, not 2 010 000).

**Feedback cue:** After you finish, check that each spoken number has exactly six zeros after the leading digit.

#### Write numbers in millions

*explanation*

A million means **1,000,000** – six zeros after the 1 (see REF-1). When we write a number in millions we are saying how many groups of 1,000,000 are present.

**Conversion rule:** to change a decimal expression such as 0.835 million into a standard numeral, move the decimal point **six places to the right**. This adds the six zeros that make up one million.

*Why this matters:* a common slip is to move the decimal the wrong way or by the wrong number of places, which leads to the place‑value misplacement seen in the diagnostic items. Always count six places: one‑hundred‑thousands, ten‑thousands, thousands, hundreds, tens, units.

*worked_example*

**Worked Example (with hints)**

1. **Convert 0.835 million**
   - **Step 1:** Write the number as a decimal – 0.835.
   - **Step 2 (Hint):** Move the decimal six places right → 835,000.
   - **Step 3:** Check the size – 835,000 is eight hundred thirty‑five thousand.
   - **Result:** 0.835 million = 835,000 (eight hundred thirty‑five thousand).

2. **Convert 20.05 million**
   - **Step 1:** Start with 20.05.
   - **Step 2 (Hint):** Move the decimal six places right → 20,050,000.
   - **Step 3:** Verify – 20,050,000 reads as twenty million fifty thousand.
   - **Result:** 20.05 million = 20,050,000 (twenty million fifty thousand).

### Candidate A
#### Writing billions in digits and words

*explanation*

A **billion** means one thousand million. In the decimal system each new group of three zeros represents a higher place value (thousands, millions, billions, …). Therefore 1 billion is written as **1,000,000,000** – the first “1” sits in the **billions** place, the next three zeros are the **millions** place, the next three are the **thousands** place, and the final three are the **units** place.

When a number contains billions, place the billions digit(s) to the left of the first comma, then write the remaining groups of three digits separated by commas. For example:

* **2 billion 350 million 120 thousand 5** → **2,350,120,005**

**Why the commas matter:** each comma marks a shift of three places. A common slip is to move the decimal point one or two places too far, leading to answers such as “7,200 billion” instead of “7.2 billion”. Remember: the number of zeros after the decimal point tells you how many places to shift. This explanation draws on the idea of grouping zeros that is introduced when learning about millions (see REF‑1).

*worked_example*

**Worked example:** Convert **0.15 billion** to digits only and write its full name.

1. **Interpret the phrase** – 0.15 billion means 0.15 × 1 billion.
2. **Write the multiplier** – 1 billion = 1,000,000,000.
3. **Multiply:**
   - 0.15 × 1,000,000,000 = 150,000,000.
   - Notice the three groups of zeros that appear because we shifted the decimal point **nine places** (three groups of three). This step highlights the place‑value shift that many learners miss.
4. **Digits:** **150,000,000**.
5. **Name:** Since the result is less than one full billion, we express it in millions: **one hundred fifty million**.

Thus, **0.15 billion = 150,000,000 = one hundred fifty million**. The step‑by‑step multiplication mirrors the counting exercises found in the textbook (REF‑2).
