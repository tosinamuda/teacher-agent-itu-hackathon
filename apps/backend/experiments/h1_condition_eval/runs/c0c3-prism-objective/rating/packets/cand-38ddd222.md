### Cubes and Cuboids: Faces, Edges, and Vertices

In everyday life you meet many objects that are solid — a dice, a gift box, a brick. A solid shape that takes up space is called a <strong>three-dimensional (3-D) shape</strong>, and each 3-D shape can be described by three special properties:

<ul>
<li>A <strong>face</strong> is a flat surface of the shape.</li>
<li>An <strong>edge</strong> is a line where two faces meet.</li>
<li>A <strong>vertex</strong> (plural: <strong>vertices</strong>) is a corner, where edges meet.</li>
</ul>

Let's use these three words on two shapes you see all the time.

<strong>The cube</strong> — think of a dice. Every face of a cube is an identical square, all the same size. To count its parts:
<ul>
<li>It has <strong>6 faces</strong> — the top, the bottom, and four around the sides.</li>
<li>It has <strong>12 edges</strong> — each of the six square faces has four edges, but every edge is shared by two faces, so 6 × 4 ÷ 2 = 12.</li>
<li>It has <strong>8 vertices</strong> — 4 on the top and 4 on the bottom.</li>
</ul>

<strong>The cuboid</strong> — think of a brick or a shoebox. Like a cube it has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices, but unlike a cube its faces are <em>rectangles</em>, and the faces come in three pairs of equal size: the two ends match, the two sides match, and the top and bottom match. A cube is actually a special cuboid where all six faces happen to be equal squares.</li></ul>

So cubes and cuboids share the same counts — <strong>6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices</strong> — but a cube has all square faces while a cuboid has rectangular faces.</p>

<strong>Example 1 — Counting the cube</strong><br>
A dice is a cube. How many faces, edges and vertices does it have?<br>
<br>
<em>Solution:</em><br>
Faces: 6 square faces (top, bottom, and four sides).<br>
Edges: 12 (count 4 around the top, 4 around the bottom, and 4 joining them).<br>
Vertices: 8 (4 on the top face, 4 on the bottom face).<br>
<br>
Answer: 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices.

<strong>Example 2 — Telling a cuboid from a cube</strong><br>
A shoebox is 30 cm long, 20 cm wide and 10 cm high. Is it a cube or a cuboid? How can you tell?<br>
<br>
<em>Solution:</em><br>
A cube needs all six faces to be equal squares. This box has faces of lengths 30×20, 30×10, and 20×10, which are rectangles of different sizes, not equal squares. So it is a <strong>cuboid</strong>. It still has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.

**Practice**

1. A sugar cube has the shape of a cube. Write down the number of faces, edges and vertices a cube has.

2. A box of cereal is a cuboid. Its top face is a rectangle measuring 8 cm by 3 cm. Explain why this box cannot be a cube.

3. A cuboid has 12 edges. Four of its faces are rectangles of size 5 cm by 3 cm, and the other two faces are squares. Which shape does this describe — a cube or a cuboid? Give a reason for your answer.

### Pyramids and Cones: Faces, Edges, and Vertices

A pyramid is a solid shape with a flat base and triangular faces that all meet at a single point called the apex. The shape of the base gives the pyramid its name. If the base is a square, it is a square pyramid (like the famous ones in Egypt); if the base is a triangle, it is a triangular pyramid.

Every pyramid shares these properties:
- It has one base, which can be any polygon (a triangle, a square, a pentagon, and so on).
- All the other faces are triangles, and they all meet at one point, the apex.
- It has edges (where two faces meet) and vertices (corners, where edges meet).

You can count the parts of a pyramid by looking at its base. A square-based pyramid has:
- a square base,
- 4 triangular faces (one for each side of the base),
- 1 apex (the point where the triangles meet),
- 5 faces in total (the base plus the 4 triangles),
- 8 edges (4 around the base and 4 sloping up to the apex),
- 5 vertices (4 at the corners of the base plus the apex).

A cone is different. A cone has a flat circular base and one curved surface that slopes up to a single point at the top, also called the apex or vertex. Because its base is a circle, a cone has no straight edges and no corners.

A cone has:
- 1 flat circular base,
- 1 curved surface (the sloping part),
- 1 vertex (the point at the top),
- 0 edges and 0 vertices on the base, because a circle has no corners.

So the big difference is this: a pyramid has straight edges and flat triangular faces, while a cone has a smooth curved surface and no edges at all.

Worked Example 1 — A triangular-based pyramid
A pyramid has a triangular base. How many faces, edges and vertices does it have?

Step 1: Count the faces. The base is 1 triangle, and one triangular face rises from each of its 3 sides. So faces = 1 base + 3 side triangles = 4 faces.

Step 2: Count the edges. There are 3 edges around the base and 3 edges sloping up to the apex. So edges = 3 + 3 = 6.

Step 3: Count the vertices. There are 3 corners on the base plus the apex at the top. So vertices = 3 + 1 = 4.

A triangular-based pyramid has 4 faces, 6 edges and 4 vertices.

Worked Example 2 — Comparing a square pyramid and a cone
A square-based pyramid and a cone stand side by side on a table.

For the square pyramid, count its faces, edges and vertices:
- Faces: 1 square base + 4 triangular sides = 5 faces.
- Edges: 4 base edges + 4 sloping edges = 8 edges.
- Vertices: 4 base corners + 1 apex = 5 vertices.

For the cone, count the same things:
- Faces: 1 flat circular base + 1 curved surface = 2 faces.
- Edges: none, because there is no straight edge where the circle meets the curved surface.
- Vertices: just 1, the point at the top.

So the pyramid has straight edges and flat triangular faces, while the cone has a curved surface and no edges. That is the key difference between them.

**Practice**

1. A pyramid has a square base. How many faces, edges and vertices does it have in total?

2. A cone has a flat circular base and one curved surface. How many faces, edges and vertices does a cone have?

3. Compare a triangular-based pyramid with a cone. Write down one thing about their edges that is different, and one thing they both have.

4. A pyramid's base has 5 sides (a pentagon). Without drawing it, work out how many faces, edges and vertices the pyramid has. Explain your counting.
