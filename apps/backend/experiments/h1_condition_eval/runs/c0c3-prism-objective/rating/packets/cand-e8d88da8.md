## The revision pack

### Cylinders and Spheres: Their Properties

Two three-dimensional shapes you will meet often are the <strong>cylinder</strong> and the <strong>sphere</strong>. Look at fig-01: a cylinder drawn upright. It has a flat circular top and a flat circular base, with a curved wall joining them. A cylinder has these properties:

1. It has <strong>two circular faces</strong> (the top and the base) and <strong>one curved face</strong>.
2. It has <strong>no vertex</strong> — there is no sharp corner anywhere.
3. It has <strong>two curved edges</strong>, where each circular face meets the curved wall.
4. It has <strong>three faces altogether</strong> — the two flat circular faces and the one curved face.

*[figure: A cylinder drawn upright: an ellipse for the top face, two vertical sides falling from its ends, and a curve closing the base.]*

Now look at fig-02: a sphere, drawn as a circle with a curved line across its middle to show that its surface is curved. Think of a ball — that is a sphere. A sphere has these properties:

1. It is <strong>symmetrical from every direction</strong> — whichever way you turn it, it looks the same.
2. It has <strong>no edge and no vertex</strong> — there is nothing sharp on a sphere at all.
3. It has <strong>one curved surface</strong> only.

*[figure: A sphere drawn as a circle with an ellipse across its middle to show the curved surface.]*

### Finding the Volume of Cubes, Cuboids and Prisms

Volume is the amount of space a three-dimensional shape takes up, and we measure it in cubic units such as cm³, m³ or mm³. To find the volume of any solid that has a flat base and straight vertical sides, we can multiply the area of the base by the height of the shape.

For a cuboid, the base is a rectangle, so the area of the base is length × breadth. Multiplying that by the height gives the volume:

Volume of a cuboid = length × breadth × height = lbh

A cube is a special cuboid where all three edges are the same length, so length = breadth = height = l. Its volume is therefore:

Volume of a cube = length × length × length = l³

Example 1. Calculate the volume of a cuboid of length 3 cm, breadth 2 cm and height 8 cm.

Solution:

Volume of a cuboid = length × breadth × height

length = 3 cm, breadth = 2 cm, height = 8 cm

Volume = 3 cm × 2 cm × 8 cm = 48 cm³

So the volume of the cuboid is 48 cm³.

Example 2. The volume of a cuboid is 24 cm³. If the base is 6 cm by 2 cm, find the height of the cuboid.

Solution:

Volume of a cuboid = length × breadth × height

length = 6 cm, breadth = 2 cm, height = ?

Volume = 24 cm³

So 24 cm³ = 6 cm × 2 cm × h

24 cm³ = 12 cm² × h

To find h, divide both sides by 12 cm²:

24 cm³ ÷ 12 cm² = h

h = 2 cm

So the height of the cuboid is 2 cm.

Example 3. Calculate the volume of a cube of side 3 cm.

Solution:

Volume of a cube = l³

V = (3 cm)³ = 3 cm × 3 cm × 3 cm = 27 cm³

So the volume of the cube is 27 cm³.

A prism is a solid with a uniform cross-section, meaning the same shape runs all the way along its length. For example, the prism in fig-03 is a triangular prism: its end is a triangle, and that triangle runs back 10 m along the prism. Its base does not have to be a rectangle, so we use the general rule:

Volume of a prism = area of base × height

Here "base" means the cross-section that is repeated, and the "height" is how far that shape runs along the prism.

*[figure: A triangular prism lying on its rectangular face. The triangular end has a base of 5 m and a height of 3 m, and the prism runs back 10 m.]*

Example 4. In fig-03, the triangular prism has a triangular end with a base of 5 m and a height of 3 m, and the prism runs back 10 m. Calculate its volume.

Solution:

First find the area of the triangular base.

Area of a triangle = ½ × base × height = ½ × 5 × 3 = 7.5 m²

Now multiply by the distance the prism runs back:

Volume of prism = area of base × height = 7.5 m² × 10 m = 75 m³

So the volume of the prism is 75 m³.
