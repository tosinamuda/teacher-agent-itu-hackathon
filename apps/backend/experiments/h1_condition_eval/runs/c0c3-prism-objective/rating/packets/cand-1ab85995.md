## The revision pack

### Getting the Order Right in Algebra

When an algebraic expression mixes different operations, you cannot just work from left to right — the order you do them in changes the answer. In algebra, just as in arithmetic, you follow a fixed order:

1. **B**rackets first — work out whatever is inside them.
2. **O**rders (powers and roots), if any.
3. **D**ivision and **M**ultiplication, from left to right.
4. **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction, from left to right.

You can remember this as **BODMAS**. The key rule for algebra is that multiplication and division are done *before* addition and subtraction. So in an expression like `4n + 3n × 10`, you do not add `4n + 3n` first — you must multiply `3n × 10` before you add anything. Brackets, when they appear, override everything and are worked out first.

**Simplify `4n + 3n × 10`.**

There are no brackets, so multiplication comes first.

- Multiply first: `3n × 10 = 30n`
- Now add: `4n + 30n = 34n`

So `4n + 3n × 10 = 34n`. If you had wrongly added first you would have got `7n × 10 = 70n` — a very different result, so the order really matters.

**Simplify `7 × 3a − (3a + 5a) × 2`.**

Here there is a bracket, and BODMAS says it comes first.

- Work out the bracket: `3a + 5a = 8a`
- The expression is now `7 × 3a − 8a × 2`
- Multiply first: `7 × 3a = 21a` and `8a × 2 = 16a`
- Now subtract: `21a − 16a = 5a`

So `7 × 3a − (3a + 5a) × 2 = 5a`.

**Practice**

4. Simplify `36y ÷ 6 + y + 2 × 4y`.

### Removing and Inserting Brackets in Algebra

Brackets are used in algebra to group terms together. When a whole expression sits inside a bracket, everything inside must be treated as one unit. To simplify an expression containing brackets, you first remove the brackets, and then you add or subtract the like terms.

The sign that sits just in front of a bracket tells you what to do with the terms inside:

- When a <strong>plus sign</strong> sits in front of the bracket, you may remove the bracket and write each term inside with the sign it already has.
- When a <strong>minus sign</strong> sits in front of the bracket, removing the bracket changes the sign of <strong>every</strong> term inside: a plus becomes a minus, and a minus becomes a plus.

Think of it this way: subtracting a group is the same as subtracting each member of that group one by one. So $-(a + b)$ means $-a - b$, and $-(a - b)$ means $-a + b$.

Once the brackets are gone, you simplify by collecting like terms — terms that have exactly the same letter. Add or subtract their numbers and keep the letter unchanged.

<strong>Example 1:</strong> Simplify $(12a - 7) + (4 - 5a)$.

A plus sign is in front of each bracket, so we remove the brackets and keep every sign the same:

$$(12a - 7) + (4 - 5a) = 12a - 7 + 4 - 5a$$

Now collect like terms. The terms in $a$ are $12a$ and $-5a$, and the plain numbers are $-7$ and $4$:

$$12a - 5a - 7 + 4 = 7a - 3$$

So $(12a - 7) + (4 - 5a) = 7a - 3$.

<strong>Example 2:</strong> Simplify $(8x - 2y) - (9x - 4y)$.

The first bracket has a plus sign in front, so its terms stay as they are. The second bracket has a <strong>minus sign</strong> in front, so every sign inside it must change: $9x$ becomes $-9x$ and $-4y$ becomes $+4y$.

$$(8x - 2y) - (9x - 4y) = 8x - 2y - 9x + 4y$$

Collect the like terms:

$$8x - 9x - 2y + 4y = -x + 2y$$

So $(8x - 2y) - (9x - 4y) = -x + 2y$, which is often written $2y - x$.

Inserting a bracket is the reverse process. When you need to put brackets around part of an expression, you work out what sign must go in front of the bracket so that the expression inside changes correctly.

- To insert a bracket with a <strong>plus sign</strong> in front, simply write the bracket and keep every term's sign the same as it was.
- To insert a bracket with a <strong>minus sign</strong> in front, every term that goes inside must have its sign <em>changed</em> — because removing that bracket later would flip the signs back to what you started with.

For example, suppose you want to factor a pair of terms out of $10a - 5b - 3$ so that the last two terms sit in a bracket after a minus sign. You want to write

$$10a - (5b + 3)$$

Check it: removing the bracket gives $10a - 5b - 3$, exactly the original expression. Because the minus sign is in front, the $5b$ and the $3$ both had their signs changed (both were plus in the bracket, and both come out as minus). That is how you know the bracket was inserted correctly.

**Practice**

1. Simplify $12 - (7 - 2f) - (3 - f)$.

2. Simplify $(8a - 5) + (14 - 3a)$.

3. Insert a bracket around the last two terms of $14a - 6b - 4$ so that a minus sign stands in front of the bracket, keeping the value the same.

### Turning Word Problems into Algebraic Expressions

A word problem gives you information in sentences, and your job is to turn those sentences into algebra before you do any simplifying. The key is to read carefully and decide what each part means.

Here is how to translate common phrases:

- "A pen costs h naira" — the unknown quantity h stands for the pen's price.
- "20 less than a pen" — take the pen's price and subtract 20, so it becomes h − 20.
- "A crayon costs ₦20 less than a pen" — the crayon costs h − 20 naira.
- "The total cost of the two" — add the two costs together.
- "3 more than something" — add 3; "3 less than something" — subtract 3.
- "twice a number" — multiply by 2; "half of a number" — divide by 2.

So a sentence like "a pencil costs ₦5 less than a pen that costs h naira" becomes h − 5. Once you have written the algebra, you simplify it the same way you have been doing all along.

Example 1
A pen costs h naira and a crayon costs ₦20 less than a pen.

(a) Find the cost of a crayon.
The crayon costs ₦20 less than the pen, so subtract 20 from the pen's price:
Cost of a crayon = h − 20 naira.

(b) Find the total cost of a crayon and a pen.
Add the pen's cost and the crayon's cost:
Total = h + (h − 20) = h + h − 20 = 2h − 20 naira.

Example 2
Envelopes cost d naira per pack, and 4 notebooks cost ₦20 more than a pack of envelopes. Find the total cost of a pack of envelopes and the notebooks.

The envelopes cost d naira.
The notebooks cost ₦20 more than the envelopes, so they cost d + 20 naira.
Total cost = d + (d + 20) = d + d + 20 = 2d + 20 naira.

Notice in both examples that you write the phrase as an expression first, then collect the like terms to simplify.

**Practice**

1. A pencil costs ₦x and a pen costs ₦8 more than a pencil. Write an expression for the total cost of the pencil and the pen, and simplify it.

2. A packet of biscuits costs k naira and a loaf of bread costs ₦12 less than a packet of biscuits. Find, in terms of k, the total cost of the biscuit packet and the loaf of bread.

3. A pen and a pencil together cost ₦x. If the pen costs ₦y, write an expression for the cost of 3 pencils.

## Check your work

**Getting the Order Right in Algebra**

1. 34n

   Multiplication comes before addition: `3n × 10 = 30n`, then `4n + 30n = 34n`.

2. 32 + 4x

   Division comes before addition: `8x ÷ 2 = 4x`, then `32 + 4x` (the `32` and the `4x` are unlike terms, so they cannot be combined).

3. 17x

   Do the multiplication and division first, left to right: `3 × 5x = 15x` and `4x ÷ 2 = 2x`. Then add: `15x + 2x = 17x`.

4. 15y

   Do the division and multiplication first, left to right: `36y ÷ 6 = 6y` and `2 × 4y = 8y`. The expression becomes `6y + y + 8y`. Now add: `6y + y + 8y = 15y`.

5. 5a

   Work out the bracket first: `3a + 5a = 8a`. Now the expression is `7 × 3a − 8a × 2`. Multiply first: `7 × 3a = 21a` and `8a × 2 = 16a`. Then subtract: `21a − 16a = 5a`.

**Removing and Inserting Brackets in Algebra**

1. $2 + 3f$

   The first number $12$ has no bracket around it, so it stays. The first bracket has a minus sign in front, so $7 - 2f$ becomes $-7 + 2f$. The second bracket also has a minus sign in front, so $3 - f$ becomes $-3 + f$. Together:

$$12 - (7 - 2f) - (3 - f) = 12 - 7 + 2f - 3 + f$$

Collect the plain numbers $12 - 7 - 3 = 2$, and collect the $f$ terms $2f + f = 3f$:

$$12 - 7 + 2f - 3 + f = 2 + 3f$$

2. $5a + 9$

   A plus sign is in front of each bracket, so remove them and keep every sign the same:

$$(8a - 5) + (14 - 3a) = 8a - 5 + 14 - 3a$$

Collect the $a$ terms $8a - 3a = 5a$, and collect the numbers $-5 + 14 = 9$:

$$8a - 5 + 14 - 3a = 5a + 9$$

3. $14a - (6b + 4)$

   Write the first term $14a$ as it is, then place the bracket after the minus sign. The two terms that go inside are $-6b$ and $-4$, but because a minus sign will sit in front of the bracket, their signs must be changed inside the bracket: $-6b$ becomes $+6b$ and $-4$ becomes $+4$. So the result is

$$14a - (6b + 4)$$

Check by removing the bracket: $14a - 6b - 4$, which matches the original expression.

**Turning Word Problems into Algebraic Expressions**

1. x + (x + 8) = 2x + 8 naira

   The pen costs ₦8 more than the pencil, so the pen costs x + 8. The total is the pencil plus the pen: x + (x + 8) = x + x + 8 = 2x + 8.

2. k + (k − 12) = 2k − 12 naira

   The bread costs ₦12 less than the biscuits, so it costs k − 12. The total is k + (k − 12) = k + k − 12 = 2k − 12.

3. 3(x − y) naira

   The pen and pencil together cost x, and the pen costs y, so one pencil costs x − y. Three pencils cost 3 × (x − y) = 3(x − y) naira.
