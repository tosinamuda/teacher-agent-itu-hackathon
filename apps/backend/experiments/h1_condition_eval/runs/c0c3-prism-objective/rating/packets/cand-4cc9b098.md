## The revision pack

### Cubes and Cuboids: Counting Faces, Edges and Vertices

Some shapes are solid: they have length, breadth and height, so they take up space. These are called **three-dimensional** shapes or **solid shapes**. A cube of sugar, a drink can and a match box are all solid shapes.

To describe a solid shape we talk about its **faces**, its **edges** and its **vertices** (one is a *vertex*).

- A **face** (or surface) is a flat or curved side of the shape.
- An **edge** is a line where two faces meet. It may be straight or curved.
- A **vertex** is a point corner where edges meet.

In this section you will learn the properties of a **cube** and a **cuboid** — that is, how many faces, edges and vertices each one has.

A **cube** is the solid shape you know from a cube of sugar. All of its faces are squares, and they are all the same size.

Look at fig-01, which shows a cube drawn as a see-through solid. Count its parts and you will find these properties:

1. It is a solid shape.
2. It has **6 square faces** — every face is a square.
3. It has **12 edges**.
4. It has **8 vertices**.

A **cuboid** is a solid shape like a match box. Its faces are rectangles, and opposite faces are the same size. Fig-02 shows a cuboid, with leader lines naming its **face**, one of its **vertices** and one of its **edges**. A cuboid has these properties:

1. It has **6 faces**.
2. It has **12 edges**.
3. It has **8 vertices**.

*[figure: A cube drawn as a see-through solid: two equal squares, the nearer one offset down and to the right of the farther one, with the four pairs of corresponding corners joined.]*

**Worked example**

Look at fig-01, the cube. Let us count its properties step by step.

- **Faces.** A face is a flat side. The cube has a top, a bottom, and four sides around it — that is 1 + 1 + 4 = **6** square faces.
- **Edges.** An edge is a line where two faces meet. Each of the two squares in fig-01 has four sides, and the four corners are joined, so the cube has 4 + 4 + 4 = **12 edges**.
- **Vertices.** A vertex is a point corner where edges meet. The two squares in fig-01 have four corners each, so the cube has 4 + 4 = **8 vertices**.

Now look at fig-02, the cuboid. It has the same number of parts: **6** faces (all rectangles), **12** edges and **8** vertices. The only difference is that a cube's faces are all squares, while a cuboid's faces are rectangles.

*[figure: A cuboid drawn the same way, wide and shallow, with three leader lines to the right naming the top face, the nearer top-right corner and the nearer right-hand vertical edge.]*

**Practice**

1. How many vertices has a cuboid?

2. How many edges has a cube?

3. How many faces has a cube, and what shape are they?

4. On a sheet of paper, draw a cube and a cuboid the way fig-01 and fig-02 are drawn. On each one, label one face, one edge and one vertex, and write how many faces, edges and vertices the shape has.

### Pyramids and Cones: Counting Faces, Edges and Vertices

A pyramid is named after the shape of its base. Look at fig-03, fig-04, fig-05 and fig-06: the triangular base pyramid (fig-03), the square base pyramid (fig-04), the pentagonal based pyramid (fig-05) and the rectangular based pyramid (fig-06). Each name tells you the shape of the base. A triangular base pyramid is also called a tetrahedron.

Every pyramid has one flat base and sloping triangular faces that all meet at a single point on top, called the apex. To describe a pyramid you count three things:

- its <strong>faces</strong> — the flat surfaces (the base plus the sloping triangles);
- its <strong>edges</strong> — the lines where two faces meet;
- its <strong>vertices</strong> — the corner points where edges meet.

The table below lists these for the four pyramids in the figures:

| Shape | Faces | Edges | Vertices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triangular based Pyramid (tetrahedron) | 4 | 6 | 4 |
| Square based Pyramid | 5 | 8 | 5 |
| Pentagonal base Pyramid | 6 | 10 | 6 |
| Rectangular based Pyramid | 5 | 8 | 5 |

*[figure: A rectangular based pyramid, drawn like the square one but on a base wider than it is tall.]*

*[figure: A pentagonal based pyramid. The base is drawn as a pentagon standing on one side with its fifth vertex uppermost, and the apex above is joined to that vertex, to the two vertices either side of it, and to the lower-right vertex — four edges from the apex, the last of them crossing the face of the solid.]*

*[figure: A square base pyramid. The base is drawn as an upright rectangle and the apex above is joined to all four of its corners, so two edges run outside the rectangle and two cross it.]*

*[figure: A triangular base pyramid. The base triangle is drawn in perspective, its third vertex raised into the middle of the figure, and the apex above is joined to all three base vertices.]*

A cone is a different solid. Look at fig-07, where the cone is drawn twice — once standing upright and once lying on its side. It does not have a set of flat triangular faces like a pyramid. Instead:

1. It has <strong>two faces</strong> — one curved face and one flat face (the circular base).
2. It has <strong>one edge</strong> — the rim where the curved face meets the flat base.
3. It has <strong>one vertex</strong> — the pointed apex at the top.

So a cone is a solid with a single curved face, a single flat face, one edge and one vertex.

*[figure: Two drawings side by side. On the left an upright cone: an elliptical base with the two slant sides running up from the ends of the ellipse to the apex. On the right a cone lying on its side, apex at the left, its two slant sides reaching a narrow upright ellipse whose top and bottom then continue as two parallel lines to a second narrow ellipse further right.]*

<strong>Worked example.</strong> How many faces, edges and vertices does a square base pyramid have?

The name tells you the base is a square (fig-04). Reading across the table, a square base pyramid has <strong>5 faces</strong> (1 square base + 4 sloping triangles), <strong>8 edges</strong> and <strong>5 vertices</strong>.

**Practice**

1. How many faces, edges and vertices does a pentagonal base pyramid have?

2. A tetrahedron has 4 faces and 6 edges. How many vertices does it have?

3. How many faces, edges and vertices does a cone have?

4. How many edges and vertices does a rectangular base pyramid have?
