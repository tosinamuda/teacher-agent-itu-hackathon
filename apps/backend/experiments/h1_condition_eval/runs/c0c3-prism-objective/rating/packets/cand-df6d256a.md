## The revision pack

### Properties of Cubes and Cuboids

A three-dimensional shape is solid, so it has three measures: length, breadth (width) and height. When we study a solid shape, we look at its faces, its edges and its vertices (corners). Learning these three things for a shape lets you recognise it and tell it apart from other shapes.

A face is a flat surface of the shape. An edge is a line where two faces meet. A vertex (plural: vertices) is a corner, where edges meet.

Let's look at a cuboid first. A cuboid is a box whose faces are rectangles — think of a brick, a book or a matchbox. Every cuboid has the same properties:
• Faces: 6
• Edges: 12
• Vertices: 8

Notice that not all the faces of a cuboid look the same, because a cuboid's faces are rectangles of different sizes. But it still always has exactly 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.

Now look at a cube. A cube is a special cuboid: every one of its faces is a square, and all the squares are the same size. Think of a dice. Because a cube is just a cuboid with equal square faces, it has the same counts:
• Faces: 6
• Edges: 12
• Vertices: 8

So both a cube and a cuboid have 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices. The difference between them is the shape of their faces: a cube's 6 faces are all equal squares, while a cuboid's faces are rectangles (not all the same).

Let's work through the properties of a cube, then a cuboid.

A cube has 6 faces. Count them: front, back, top, bottom, left and right — that is 6. Each of these faces is a square, and they are all the same size.

A cube has 12 edges. The front face has 4 edges, and the back face has 4 edges, and there are 4 edges joining the front to the back — 4 + 4 + 4 = 12.

A cube has 8 vertices — one at each corner, top and bottom, front and back: 4 on top and 4 on the bottom.

A cuboid has exactly the same counts: 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices. The only difference is the shape of its faces. A cuboid's faces are rectangles (for example a front that is taller than it is wide), and opposite faces are the same size. In a cube all six faces are equal squares.

So when you are asked for the properties of a cube or a cuboid, answer with the three counts — faces, edges and vertices — and, for a cube, add that all its faces are equal squares.

**Practice**

1. How many vertices has a cuboid?

2. How many edges has a cube?

3. A box of chalk is the shape of a cuboid. List its number of faces, edges and vertices.

4. A dice is in the shape of a cube. How many faces does it have, and what shape is each face?

### Pyramids and Cones

In the last section you looked at cubes and cuboids — shapes built from flat square and rectangular faces. Now meet two more three-dimensional figures: pyramids and cones. Both are built from a flat base that rises up to a single point, but their sides are very different.

A <strong>pyramid</strong> has a flat base that is a polygon (a triangle, a square, a pentagon, a hexagon, and so on), and every other face is a triangle. All these triangular faces meet at one point at the top, called the <strong>apex</strong> (or vertex). A pyramid is named by the shape of its base: a pyramid with a square base is a square pyramid, one with a triangular base is a triangular pyramid, and one with a hexagonal base is a hexagonal pyramid.

A <strong>cone</strong> has a flat circular base, and instead of triangular faces it has one curved surface that tapers smoothly up to a single point, the apex. So while a pyramid is made entirely of flat faces, a cone has exactly one curved face.

Let's list their properties side by side.

Here are the properties to know for each figure.

<strong>Square pyramid</strong> (a pyramid with a square base):
<ul>
<li>It has 5 faces: 1 square base and 4 triangular faces.</li>
<li>It has 8 edges.</li>
<li>It has 5 vertices: the 4 corners of the square base plus the apex.</li>
</ul>

<strong>Hexagonal pyramid</strong> (a pyramid with a hexagonal base):
<ul>
<li>It has 7 faces: 1 hexagonal base and 6 triangular faces.</li>
<li>It has 12 edges.</li>
<li>It has 7 vertices: the 6 corners of the hexagon base plus the apex.</li>
</ul>

<strong>Cone</strong>:
<ul>
<li>It has 2 faces: 1 circular base and 1 curved surface.</li>
<li>It has 0 edges (no straight edges at all).</li>
<li>It has 1 vertex: the apex.</li>
</ul>

Spot the pattern for a pyramid: if the base is a polygon with <em>n</em> sides, the pyramid has <em>n</em> triangular faces plus the 1 base (so <em>n</em> + 1 faces in total), <em>n</em> + 1 vertices, and 2<em>n</em> edges. Check it against the square pyramid: <em>n</em> = 4, so faces = 5, vertices = 5, edges = 8. That works! And the hexagon: <em>n</em> = 6, so faces = 7, vertices = 7, edges = 12.

<strong>Worked example — listing the properties of a pentagonal pyramid</strong>

A pentagonal pyramid has a pentagon (5 sides) for its base.

<em>Faces.</em> It has 1 pentagonal base and 5 triangular faces that rise to the apex. So the number of faces is 5 + 1 = 6.

<em>Vertices.</em> It has the 5 corners of the base plus the 1 apex, so 5 + 1 = 6 vertices.

<em>Edges.</em> Using the pattern, edges = 2 × <em>n</em> = 2 × 5 = 10.

Check by counting: the base is a pentagon (5 edges), and each of the 5 corners sends an edge up to the apex (5 more edges). That gives 5 + 5 = 10 edges. 

So a pentagonal pyramid has 6 faces, 6 vertices and 10 edges.

**Practice**

1. A hexagonal pyramid has a hexagon for its base. List all its properties: how many faces, how many vertices and how many edges does it have?

2. A tetrahedron is a pyramid with a triangular base. How many vertices does a tetrahedron have?

3. A cone has how many faces, edges and vertices?

**Properties of Cubes and Cuboids**

1. A cuboid has 8 vertices (corners).

   A cuboid has 4 corners at the top and 4 corners at the bottom, so 4 + 4 = 8 vertices in total.

2. A cube has 12 edges.

   A cube has 4 edges on the top face, 4 edges on the bottom face, and 4 vertical edges joining the top to the bottom. 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 edges.

3. A cuboid (the chalk box) has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.

   Every cuboid — a chalk box, a book or a brick — has the same three counts: 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.

4. A cube has 6 faces, and every face is a square (all the same size).

   A cube is a cuboid whose 6 faces are all equal squares, which is why a dice — a cube — has 6 square faces.

**Pyramids and Cones**

1. 7 faces (1 hexagonal base and 6 triangular faces), 7 vertices (6 base corners plus the apex), and 12 edges.

   The base is a hexagon, so n = 6. Faces = n + 1 = 7. Vertices = n + 1 = 7. Edges = 2n = 2 × 6 = 12.

2. 4 vertices: the 3 corners of the triangular base plus the 1 apex.

   n = 3 for a triangle. Vertices = n + 1 = 3 + 1 = 4.

3. 2 faces (1 circular base and 1 curved surface), 0 edges, and 1 vertex (the apex).
