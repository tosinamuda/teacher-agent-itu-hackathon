## The revision pack

### Cubes and Cuboids: Faces, Edges and Vertices

When we talk about a solid shape like a cube or a cuboid, we describe it by counting its faces, edges and vertices.

- A <strong>face</strong> is a flat surface of the shape.
- An <strong>edge</strong> is the line where two faces meet.
- A <strong>vertex</strong> (plural: vertices) is a corner where edges meet.

<h3>The cube</h3>
A cube is a solid in which every face is a square of the same size.

- Faces: 6 (all squares, and they are all equal).
- Edges: 12 (and they are all the same length).
- Vertices: 8.

Because every edge of a cube is the same length, the cube is the same width, depth and height.

<h3>The cuboid</h3>
A cuboid is a solid whose faces are rectangles. It has the same counts as a cube, but its edges are not all the same length.

- Faces: 6 (rectangles). Opposite faces are equal, so the six faces come in three equal pairs.
- Edges: 12. They come in three groups of four, and the four edges in each group are equal.
- Vertices: 8.

A cuboid has a length, a width and a height, and those three measurements are usually different. If you imagine making all three equal, the cuboid becomes a cube — that is why a cube is a special kind of cuboid.

<h3>Worked example — labelling a cuboid</h3>
Here is how you check and label a cuboid before you answer any question about it.

Imagine the box of a shoe. Its top, its base and its four sides are all rectangles. Counting them:

- The top is one face and the base is another face, so that is 2.
- The four sides are 4 more faces.
- Total faces = 6. ✓

Now look at one corner of the box: three edges meet there. Count the edges around the box — top has 4, base has 4, and the four vertical edges join them — that gives 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 edges. ✓

Finally, each corner of the box is a vertex. There are 4 corners on top and 4 on the bottom, so the box has 8 vertices. ✓

So a cuboid always has <strong>6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices</strong>, and its opposite faces are equal.

### Pyramids and Cones: Getting to Know Their Properties

In this section you will learn to identify the properties of pyramids and cones. Just as you did for cubes and cuboids, you will meet each shape, learn what it is made of, and list its properties — the number of faces, edges and vertices, and the shapes of those faces.

A pyramid is a three-dimensional shape with a flat base that is a polygon (a shape with straight sides) and triangular faces that all meet at a single point at the top, called the apex. The name of a pyramid tells you the shape of its base.

Let's look at two pyramids.

A square-based pyramid has a square base. Its properties are:
- It has 5 faces: 1 square base and 4 triangular faces.
- It has 8 edges.
- It has 5 vertices.

A triangular-based pyramid has a triangular base. Its properties are:
- It has 4 faces: 1 triangular base and 3 triangular faces.
- It has 6 edges.
- It has 4 vertices.

A triangular-based pyramid is also called a tetrahedron. Notice its name: "tetra" means four, and it has 4 faces. When someone asks how many vertices a tetrahedron has, the answer is 4.

A cone is a three-dimensional shape with a flat circular base and a curved surface that narrows to a single point, called the apex. Its properties are:
- It has 2 faces: 1 flat circular base and 1 curved surface.
- It has 1 vertex (the point at the top).
- It has no edges, because the base is a circle and the curved surface has no straight edge.

So when you list the properties of a cone, remember it has one curved surface and one flat circular base, one vertex at its apex, and no edges.

The key difference between a pyramid and a cone is the base: a pyramid has a polygonal (straight-sided) base, while a cone has a circular base.

Let's work through an example together: an hexagonal base pyramid.

An hexagonal base pyramid has a base that is a hexagon (a six-sided polygon), and each side of the hexagon has one triangular face rising to the apex. So:

- Faces: 1 hexagonal base + 6 triangular faces = 7 faces.
- Edges: 6 edges around the hexagonal base + 6 edges from the base up to the apex = 12 edges.
- Vertices: the 6 vertices around the base + 1 apex = 7 vertices.

So an hexagonal base pyramid has 7 faces, 12 edges and 7 vertices.

Now try these for yourself.
