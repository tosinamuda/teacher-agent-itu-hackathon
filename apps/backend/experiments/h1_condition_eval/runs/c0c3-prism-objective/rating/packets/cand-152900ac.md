## The revision pack

### Order of Operations in Algebra

When an algebraic expression contains several operations, you must do them in a fixed order so that everyone gets the same answer. Work out any **brackets** first, then do **multiplication and division** from left to right, and finally **addition and subtraction** from left to right.

In algebra, remember that a term like <span>$3x$</span> means <span>$3 \times x$</span>. So <span>$3x \times 2$</span> really means <span>$3 \times x \times 2$</span>, and <span>$17y - 5 \times 2y$</span> means you must do the multiplication <span>$5 \times 2y$</span> before you subtract. Likewise, <span>$7x \div x$</span> means <span>$7 \times x \div x$</span>, which simplifies because the <span>$x$</span>'s cancel.

**Example 1 — finding values in order**

(a) <span>$18 - 6 \times 2$</span>

Multiply first: <span>$6 \times 2 = 12$</span>, then subtract.

<span>$18 - 12 = 6$</span>

(b) <span>$6 - 18 \div 3$</span>

Divide first: <span>$18 \div 3 = 6$</span>, then subtract.

<span>$6 - 6 = 0$</span>

(c) <span>$6 \times 2 - 2 - 40 \div 4$</span>

Do the multiplication and division first: <span>$6 \times 2 = 12$</span> and <span>$40 \div 4 = 10$</span>, then subtract left to right.

<span>$12 - 2 - 10 = 10 - 10 = 0$</span>

**Example 2 — simplifying expressions**

(a) <span>$3x \times 2 + 5x$</span>

Multiply first: <span>$3x \times 2 = 6x$</span>. Then add the like terms.

<span>$6x + 5x = 11x$</span>

(b) <span>$17y - 5 \times 2y$</span>

Multiply first: <span>$5 \times 2y = 10y$</span>. Then subtract the like terms.

<span>$17y - 10y = 7y$</span>

**Example 3 — division and brackets**

(a) <span>$7x \div x + 5$</span>

Divide first: <span>$7x \div x = 7$</span> (the <span>$x$</span>'s cancel). Then add.

<span>$7 + 5 = 12$</span>

(b) <span>$4x - (6 \div 3)$</span>

Work inside the bracket first: <span>$6 \div 3 = 2$</span>. Then subtract.

<span>$4x - 2$</span>

(c) <span>$(24x \div 6) + x + (1 \times 5x)$</span>

Work the brackets first: <span>$24x \div 6 = 4x$</span> and <span>$1 \times 5x = 5x$</span>. Then add the like terms.

<span>$4x + x + 5x = 5x + 5x = 10x$</span>

### Removing Brackets and Simplifying Expressions

When an algebraic expression has brackets, you often want to remove them so you can collect like terms. Two rules tell you exactly what happens when the bracket comes off.

<strong>Rule 1 — a <span class="math">+</span> sign in front of a bracket.</strong> A plus sign before a bracket does not change any sign inside the bracket. Every term inside keeps its own sign as the bracket is removed.

<strong>Rule 2 — a <span class="math">−</span> sign in front of a bracket.</strong> A minus sign before a bracket changes every sign inside it: each <span class="math">+</span> becomes <span class="math">−</span>, and each <span class="math">−</span> becomes <span class="math">+</span>.

Before you start, always check whether the terms inside the bracket can be simplified first — if they can, do that first, then remove the bracket.

<strong>Example 1 — removing a bracket after a <span class="math">+</span></strong>

Simplify <span class="math">6x + (3 - x)</span>.

The <span class="math">+</span> before the bracket keeps the signs unchanged:
<span class="math">6x + (3 - x) = 6x + 3 - x</span>

Now collect the like terms:
<span class="math">6x - x + 3 = 5x + 3</span>

So <span class="math">6x + (3 - x) = 5x + 3</span>.

<strong>Example 2 — removing a bracket after a <span class="math">−</span></strong>

Simplify <span class="math">10d - (9c - 4d)</span>.

The <span class="math">−</span> before the bracket flips every sign inside: <span class="math">+9c</span> becomes <span class="math">-9c</span> and <span class="math">-4d</span> becomes <span class="math">+4d</span>.
<span class="math">10d - (9c - 4d) = 10d - 9c + 4d</span>

Collect the like terms:
<span class="math">10d + 4d - 9c = 14d - 9c</span>

So <span class="math">10d - (9c - 4d) = 14d - 9c</span>.

<strong>Example 3 — two brackets</strong>

Simplify <span class="math">(6x - y) - (4x - 5y)</span>.

Remove the first bracket: the <span class="math">+</span> in front of it keeps signs unchanged, so it stays <span class="math">6x - y</span>.

Remove the second bracket: the <span class="math">−</span> flips each sign, so <span class="math">4x</span> becomes <span class="math">-4x</span> and <span class="math">-5y</span> becomes <span class="math">+5y</span>.
<span class="math">6x - y - 4x + 5y</span>

Collect the like terms:
<span class="math">6x - 4x + 5y - y = 2x + 4y = 2(x + 2y)</span>

So <span class="math">(6x - y) - (4x - 5y) = 2x + 4y</span>, or <span class="math">2(x + 2y)</span>.

### Turning Word Problems into Algebraic Expressions

A word problem describes a situation in words, and your job is to turn those words into an algebraic expression. To do this, you decide what each unknown letter stands for, then use the words to build an expression step by step.

Two phrases come up again and again:
<ul>
<li><strong>"...less than a..."</strong> means you <em>subtract</em>. If a pen costs <em>P</em> naira and a pencil costs 15 naira <em>less</em>, the pencil costs ₦(<em>P</em> − 15).</li>
<li><strong>"...more than a..."</strong> means you <em>add</em>. If a pencil costs <em>x</em> naira and a rubber costs 40 naira <em>more</em>, the rubber costs ₦(<em>x</em> + 40).</li>
</ul>

Once you have each separate cost as an expression, you add them together and then simplify by collecting like terms. When an item "joins" a group, add it; when an item "leaves", subtract it.</li>

<strong>Example 1</strong>

A pen costs ₦<em>P</em> and a pencil costs 15 naira less than a pen.

(a) Find the cost of a pencil.
(b) Find the cost of a pen and a pencil.

<strong>Solution</strong>

Cost of a pen = ₦<em>P</em>

(a) A pencil is 15 naira less than a pen, so subtract 15:
cost of a pencil = ₦(<em>P</em> − 15)

(b) To find both together, add the two costs:
cost of a pen and a pencil = ₦<em>P</em> + ₦(<em>P</em> − 15)
= ₦<em>P</em> + ₦<em>P</em> − ₦15
= ₦2<em>P</em> − ₦15
= ₦(2<em>P</em> − 15)

<strong>Example 2</strong>

A pencil costs ₦<em>x</em> and a rubber costs 40 naira more than a pencil.

(a) Find the cost of a rubber.
(b) Find the cost of a pencil and a rubber.

<strong>Solution</strong>

(a) Cost of a pencil = ₦<em>x</em>. A rubber is 40 naira more, so add 40:
cost of a rubber = ₦(<em>x</em> + 40)

(b) Add the two costs:
cost of a pencil and a rubber = ₦<em>x</em> + ₦(<em>x</em> + 40)
= ₦(<em>x</em> + <em>x</em> + 40)
= ₦(2<em>x</em> + 40)

<strong>Example 3</strong>

In a class there are <em>x</em> boys and <em>y</em> girls. This semester, 5 boys join and 3 girls leave the class. How many students are now in this class?

<strong>Solution</strong>

Total number = number of boys + number of girls.

5 boys join, so the number of boys becomes <em>x</em> + 5.
3 girls leave, so the number of girls becomes <em>y</em> − 3.

Total number = (<em>x</em> + 5) + (<em>y</em> − 3)
= <em>x</em> + <em>y</em> + 5 − 3
= <em>x</em> + <em>y</em> + 2

So there are now (<em>x</em> + <em>y</em> + 2) students in the class.

## Check your work

**Order of Operations in Algebra**

1. $34n$

   Multiply first: $3n \times 10 = 30n$. Then add the like terms: $4n + 30n = 34n$.

2. $32 + 4x$

   Divide first: $8x \div 2 = 4x$. Then add: $32 + 4x$. The terms are not alike, so they cannot be combined further.

3. $17x$

   Do the multiplication and division first: $3 \times 5x = 15x$ and $4x \div 2 = 2x$. Then add the like terms: $15x + 2x = 17x$.

4. $15y$

   Do the division and multiplication first: $36y \div 6 = 6y$ and $2 \times 4y = 8y$. Then add the like terms: $6y + y + 8y = 15y$.

**Removing Brackets and Simplifying Expressions**

1. $7a - 3$

   Remove the bracket: the $+$ before it keeps every sign unchanged, so $12a - 7 + 4 - 5a$. Collect like terms: $12a - 5a = 7a$ and $-7 + 4 = -3$. So the expression is $7a - 3$.

2. $-x + 2y$

   Remove the first bracket (a $+$ before it): $8x - 2y$. Remove the second bracket (a $-$ before it): each sign flips, so $-(9x - 4y)$ becomes $-9x + 4y$. Together: $8x - 2y - 9x + 4y$. Collect like terms: $8x - 9x = -x$ and $-2y + 4y = 2y$. So the expression is $-x + 2y$.

3. $2 + 3f$

   Remove the first bracket (a $-$ before it): $12 - 7 + 2f$. Remove the second bracket (also a $-$ before it): $-(3 - f)$ becomes $-3 + f$. Together: $12 - 7 + 2f - 3 + f$. Collect like terms: the numbers give $12 - 7 - 3 = 2$, and the $f$ terms give $2f + f = 3f$. So the expression is $2 + 3f$.

4. $5a + 9$

   Remove the bracket (a $+$ before it keeps every sign unchanged): $8a - 5 + 14 - 3a$. Collect like terms: $8a - 3a = 5a$ and $-5 + 14 = 9$. So the expression is $5a + 9$.

**Turning Word Problems into Algebraic Expressions**

1. (a) ₦(<em>h</em> − 20)   (b) ₦(2<em>h</em> − 20)

   A crayon is 20 naira less than a pen, so subtract 20 from the pen's cost: crayon = ₦(<em>h</em> − 20). Together, pen + crayon = ₦<em>h</em> + ₦(<em>h</em> − 20) = ₦<em>h</em> + ₦<em>h</em> − ₦20 = ₦2<em>h</em> − ₦20 = ₦(2<em>h</em> − 20).

2. ₦(2<em>d</em> + 20)

   A notepad is 20 naira more than a pack of envelopes, so it costs ₦(<em>d</em> + 20). Total = pack of envelopes + notepad = ₦<em>d</em> + ₦(<em>d</em> + 20) = ₦(<em>d</em> + <em>d</em> + 20) = ₦(2<em>d</em> + 20).

3. ₦3(<em>x</em> − <em>y</em>)

   A pen and a pencil cost ₦<em>x</em>, and the pen costs ₦<em>y</em>, so one pencil costs ₦<em>x</em> − ₦<em>y</em> = ₦(<em>x</em> − <em>y</em>). Three pencils cost 3 × ₦(<em>x</em> − <em>y</em>) = ₦3(<em>x</em> − <em>y</em>).
