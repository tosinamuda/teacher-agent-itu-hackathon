### Cubes and Cuboids: Faces, Edges and Vertices

Three-dimensional shapes are shapes that have length, breadth and height. Because they have all three, they are called solid shapes. Some of them are regular, like a cube of sugar, a can drink or a match box; others are irregular, like a stone or a tree.

To describe any solid shape, you use three words:

- A <strong>face</strong> (or surface) is a flat or curved side of the shape.
- An <strong>edge</strong> is a line where two faces meet. It may be straight or curved.
- A <strong>vertex</strong> is a point corner where edges meet. The plural is <strong>vertices</strong>.

Count these parts on the cube in fig-01 and you will find its properties.

*[figure: A cube drawn as a see-through solid: two equal squares, the nearer one offset down and to the right of the farther one, with the four pairs of corresponding corners joined.]*

<strong>Properties of a cube</strong>

A cube is a solid whose every face is a square. Look at the cube drawn in fig-01 — two equal squares joined by four straight edges.

1. It is a solid shape.
2. It has <strong>6 square faces</strong>.
3. It has <strong>12 edges</strong>.
4. It has <strong>8 vertices</strong>.

<strong>Properties of a cuboid</strong>

A cuboid is like a cube but its faces are rectangles, so it can be wide and shallow rather than equal in every direction. The cuboid in fig-02 shows you a face, a vertex and an edge.

1. It has <strong>6 faces</strong>.
2. It has <strong>12 edges</strong>.
3. It has <strong>8 vertices</strong>.

So a cube and a cuboid both have 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices. They differ in their faces: every face of a cube is a square, while the faces of a cuboid are rectangles.

*[figure: A cuboid drawn the same way, wide and shallow, with three leader lines to the right naming the top face, the nearer top-right corner and the nearer right-hand vertical edge.]*

Let's check that you can find these properties on the picture. Take the cube in fig-01. Run your eye over it: count the squares that make it up — there are 6. Count the straight lines where two squares meet — there are 12 of them. Count the point corners where three edges meet — there are 8. These three numbers, 6, 12 and 8, are the properties you should always be able to recall for both a cube and a cuboid.

### Properties of Pyramids and Cones

A pyramid is a solid shape that narrows from a flat base up to a single point called the <strong>apex</strong>. A pyramid is named according to the shape of its base. For example, a pyramid whose base is a triangle is a triangular based pyramid, and one whose base is a square is a square based pyramid. Because the name always tells you the base, you can work out many of the properties without drawing anything.

Three things describe a solid shape:

- <strong>Faces</strong> — the flat surfaces that make up the shape.
- <strong>Edges</strong> — the lines where two faces meet.
- <strong>Vertices</strong> — the corners where edges meet (one corner is a vertex).

Here are the properties of four pyramids. Notice that the numbers depend on the base.

| Shape | Faces | Edges | Vertices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triangular based pyramid (tetrahedron) | 4 | 6 | 4 |
| Square based pyramid | 5 | 8 | 5 |
| Pentagonal based pyramid | 6 | 10 | 6 |
| Rectangular based pyramid | 5 | 8 | 5 |

A triangular based pyramid has a triangle for its base, so it has four faces altogether: the triangular base plus three triangular sides. It is also called a <strong>tetrahedron</strong>.

Now consider the cone. A cone has a circular flat base and a curved face that rises to a point, the apex.

Its properties are:

- It has <strong>two faces</strong> — one curved face and one flat face (the circular base).
- It has <strong>one edge</strong> — the circular rim where the curved face meets the flat base.
- It has <strong>one vertex</strong> — the pointed apex.

So unlike a pyramid, a cone has only one flat face and only one vertex, because its side is curved rather than made of flat faces.

<strong>Worked example — naming and listing properties</strong>

A solid has a pentagon for its base and a single apex above it. Name the solid and list its faces, edges and vertices.

<strong>Step 1.</strong> The name comes from the base. The base is a pentagon, so this is a <strong>pentagonal based pyramid</strong>.

<strong>Step 2.</strong> Look up its properties. From the table, a pentagonal based pyramid has 6 faces, 10 edges and 6 vertices.

<strong>Answer:</strong> Pentagonal based pyramid — 6 faces, 10 edges, 6 vertices.
