## The revision pack

### Cylinders and Spheres: Their Properties

In this section you will learn to identify the properties of two solid shapes: the cylinder and the sphere. You already know the cube, cuboid, pyramid and cone; now we look at shapes whose faces curve.

A **cylinder** looks like a tin can or a drum. Look at the cylinder in fig-01: it has a circular top, a circular base, and a curved side joining them. From the figure you can read off its properties:

1. It has **two circular faces** (the top and the base) and **one curved face**.
2. It has **no vertex** (no corner where edges meet).
3. It has **two curved edges** (the circles where the curved face meets the top face and the base).
4. Altogether it has **three faces**.

A **sphere** looks like a ball. Look at the sphere in fig-02. Its properties are:

1. It is **symmetrical from all directions** — turn it any way and it looks the same.
2. It has **no edge** and **no vertex**.
3. It has **one curved surface**.

*[figure: A sphere drawn as a circle with an ellipse across its middle to show the curved surface.]*

*[figure: A cylinder drawn upright: an ellipse for the top face, two vertical sides falling from its ends, and a curve closing the base.]*

Let us compare a cylinder and a sphere side by side.

**Example.** Count the faces, edges and vertices of a cylinder.

In fig-01 you can see the two circular ends and the curved side between them. So the cylinder has:

- Faces: two circular faces plus one curved face = **3 faces**
- Edges: two curved edges (where the curved face meets each circular face)
- Vertices: none

**Example.** Count the faces, edges and vertices of a sphere.

In fig-02 the sphere has only its curved surface. So:

- Faces: **1 curved face**
- Edges: none
- Vertices: none

Notice the difference: the cylinder has two curved edges and no vertex, while the sphere has no edge at all.

**Practice**

1. How many circular faces does a solid cylinder have?

2. A cylinder has how many faces altogether?

3. How many vertices does a sphere have?

4. Name two properties that tell a cylinder apart from a sphere.

### Volume of Cubes, Cuboids and Prisms

The volume of a solid tells you how much space it takes up. You already know the units — when you measure in centimetres, volume is in cubic centimetres (cm³), and when you measure in metres, it is in cubic metres (m³).

For a cuboid, the volume is found by multiplying the area of its base by its height:

Volume of cuboid = area of base × height
                 = length × breadth × height
                 = lbh

Remember that length, breadth and height are all measured in the same unit, and you multiply them together.

A cube is a special cuboid where every edge is the same length, l. So all three dimensions equal l, and

Volume of cube = l × l × l = l³

You can read l³ as "l cubed".

The same idea — volume equals area of base × height — also works for a prism. A prism has two identical parallel faces joined by a constant cross-section. So for any prism:

Volume of prism = area of base × height

You first find the area of the base face, then multiply it by the height (the distance the prism runs).

**Example 1.** Find the volume of a cuboid of length 3 cm, breadth 2 cm and height 8 cm.

Volume of cuboid = L × b × h

length = 3 cm, breadth = 2 cm, height = 8 cm

Volume = 3 cm × 2 cm × 8 cm
       = 48 cm³

So the cuboid has volume 48 cm³.

**Example 2.** A cuboid has volume 24 cm³. Its base is 6 cm by 2 cm. Find its height.

Volume of cuboid = L × b × h

length = 6 cm, breadth = 2 cm, height = ?

Volume = 24 cm³

24 cm³ = 6 cm × 2 cm × h
24 cm³ = 12 cm² × h

Divide both sides by 12 cm²:

24 cm³ ÷ 12 cm² = h
2 cm = h

So the height is 2 cm.

**Example 3.** Find the volume of a cube of side 3 cm.

Volume of cube = l³

V = (3 cm)³
V = 3 cm × 3 cm × 3 cm
V = 27 cm³

So the cube has volume 27 cm³.

**Example 4.** Find the volume of the triangular prism. Its triangular end has a base of 5 m and a height of 3 m, and the prism runs back 10 m.

First find the area of the triangular base:

Area of triangle = ½ × b × h
                = ½ × 5 × 3
                = 7.5 m²

Then volume = area of base × height
            = 7.5 m² × 10 m
            = 75 m³

So the prism has volume 75 m³.

*[figure: A triangular prism lying on its rectangular face. The triangular end has a base of 5 m and a height of 3 m, and the prism runs back 10 m.]*

3. What is the volume of a cube of side 4 cm?

4. A rectangular tank is 6 m long and 2 m wide, and it holds 36 m³ of water. How deep is the water in the tank?

**Cylinders and Spheres: Their Properties**

1. Two.

   A solid cylinder has a circular top and a circular base. Each is a circular face, so there are 2 circular faces.

2. Three faces (two circular faces and one curved face).

   The cylinder has two circular faces (top and base) plus one curved face joining them. So altogether it has 2 + 1 = 3 faces.

3. None — a sphere has no vertex and no edge.

   A sphere is only a curved surface, so it has no corners and no edges.

4. A cylinder has two circular faces and two curved edges; a sphere has one curved surface and no edges.

   The cylinder has faces that are circles and two curved edges, while the sphere has only a curved surface with no edges and no vertices.

**Volume of Cubes, Cuboids and Prisms**

1. 96 cm³

   Volume = L × b × h = 8 cm × 4 cm × 3 cm = 96 cm³.

2. 30 m³

   Volume = L × b × h = 5 m × 2 m × 3 m = 30 m³.

3. 64 cm³

   Volume of cube = l³ = 4 cm × 4 cm × 4 cm = 64 cm³.

4. 3 m

   Volume = L × b × h, so 36 m³ = 6 m × 2 m × h = 12 m² × h. Dividing both sides by 12 m² gives h = 36 m³ ÷ 12 m² = 3 m.
