### Following the Order of Operations in Algebra

When you simplify an algebraic expression, you must deal with its parts in a fixed order. Just as with numbers, the rule is: work out anything inside brackets first, then deal with powers, then do multiplication and division from left to right, and finally do addition and subtraction from left to right. A handy way to remember the order is <strong>BODMAS</strong>: Brackets, Of (or powers), Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction.

The letters stand for:
<ul>
<li><strong>B</strong>rackets – clear any brackets first.</li>
<li><strong>O</strong>rder / <strong>O</strong>f – handle powers like squares and cubes.</li>
<li><strong>D</strong>ivision and <strong>M</strong>ultiplication – do these from left to right as they appear.</li>
<li><strong>A</strong>ddition and <strong>S</strong>ubtraction – do these from left to right last.</li>
</ul>

The same order works whether the letters are numbers or symbols, so an expression like <strong>2x + 3 × x</strong> is not simplified from left to right blindly – the multiplication <strong>3 × x = 3x</strong> comes before the addition, giving <strong>2x + 3x = 5x</strong>. Keep this order in mind and every expression will come out the same way every time.</p>

<p><strong>Worked Example 1 – no brackets</strong><br>
Simplify <strong>4x + 2 × 3x</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Step 1:</strong> Do the multiplication first. <strong>2 × 3x = 6x</strong>.<br>
<strong>Step 2:</strong> Now add. <strong>4x + 6x = 10x</strong>.<br>
<strong>Answer:</strong> 10x</p>

<p><strong>Worked Example 2 – with brackets</strong><br>
Simplify <strong>3(2x + 4)</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Step 1:</strong> Deal with the bracket first by multiplying each term inside by the number outside. <strong>3 × 2x = 6x</strong> and <strong>3 × 4 = 12</strong>.<br>
<strong>Step 2:</strong> Write the result: <strong>6x + 12</strong>.<br>
<strong>Answer:</strong> 6x + 12</p>

<p><strong>Worked Example 3 – brackets inside a longer expression</strong><br>
Simplify <strong>2x + 4(3x − 5)</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Step 1:</strong> Multiply out the bracket first. <strong>4 × 3x = 12x</strong> and <strong>4 × (−5) = −20</strong>, so the bracket becomes <strong>12x − 20</strong>.<br>
<strong>Step 2:</strong> Bring the rest down: <strong>2x + 12x − 20</strong>.<br>
<strong>Step 3:</strong> Collect like terms. <strong>2x + 12x = 14x</strong>, so we get <strong>14x − 20</strong>.<br>
<strong>Answer:</strong> 14x − 20</p>

<p>Let's see how the order of operations applies when you have several different steps in one expression.</p>

<p><strong>Worked Example 4 – multiplication and addition together</strong><br>
Simplify <strong>5x − 2 × 3x + 4</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Step 1:</strong> Multiplication first. <strong>2 × 3x = 6x</strong>.<br>
<strong>Step 2:</strong> Replace and simplify from left to right: <strong>5x − 6x + 4</strong>.<br>
<strong>Step 3:</strong> <strong>5x − 6x = −1x</strong>, or <strong>−x</strong>, so we have <strong>−x + 4</strong>.<br>
<strong>Answer:</strong> −x + 4</p>

<p><strong>Worked Example 5 – a bracket containing two terms</strong><br>
Simplify <strong>2(4x + 3) − 5x</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Step 1:</strong> Multiply out the bracket: <strong>2 × 4x = 8x</strong> and <strong>2 × 3 = 6</strong>, giving <strong>8x + 6</strong>.<br>
<strong>Step 2:</strong> Bring down the rest: <strong>8x + 6 − 5x</strong>.<br>
<strong>Step 3:</strong> Collect like terms: <strong>8x − 5x = 3x</strong>, so we have <strong>3x + 6</strong>.<br>
<strong>Answer:</strong> 3x + 6</p>

<p>When a negative sign sits in front of a bracket, it multiplies every term inside by −1. For example, <strong>3x − (2x + 1)</strong> becomes <strong>3x − 2x − 1 = x − 1</strong>. Take care to change the sign of each term inside.</p>

<p><strong>Worked Example 6 – a bracket with a minus in front</strong><br>
Simplify <strong>4x − (2x + 3)</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Step 1:</strong> The minus in front means <strong>−1 × (2x + 3) = −2x − 3</strong>.<br>
<strong>Step 2:</strong> Bring down the front term: <strong>4x − 2x − 3</strong>.<br>
<strong>Step 3:</strong> <strong>4x − 2x = 2x</strong>, so we get <strong>2x − 3</strong>.<br>
<strong>Answer:</strong> 2x − 3</p>

### Working with Brackets in Algebra

In algebra, a bracket groups terms together and tells you to treat everything inside it as a single unit. Two skills matter here: taking a bracket away (removing or expanding it) and putting one in (factorising or inserting it). Both rely on one idea — the sign that sits in front of the bracket decides what happens to every sign inside.

<b>Removing a bracket (expanding).</b> The term just in front of the bracket multiplies every term inside, one by one. Watch the sign in front:

• If the sign in front is <b>+</b>, the signs inside stay exactly as they are.
• If the sign in front is <b>−</b>, every sign inside flips: + becomes − and − becomes +.
• If a number or term stands in front (like 3 or 2x), multiply that with each term inside as well as applying the sign rule.

For example, to remove the bracket in 3(a + b): multiply 3 by a and by b, keeping the +, to get 3a + 3b. To remove the bracket in −(c − d): the minus flips the signs, so −c + d.

<b>Putting a bracket in (inserting).</b> You can gather terms that share a common factor and write the common factor in front of a bracket. Find the biggest factor common to every term, pull it out front, and divide each term by it to see what goes inside.

For example, 4x + 4y shares a factor of 4, so it becomes 4(x + y). And −6a + 6b shares a factor of 6 (the signs are different, so the bracket carries the −): −6a + 6b = −6(a − b).

Always simplify after removing or inserting a bracket. <b>Simplification</b> means combining like terms — terms with exactly the same letters and powers — by adding or subtracting their number parts (their coefficients). Terms that do not match cannot be combined.

Here is the full sequence to follow for any expression with brackets:
1. Remove any brackets, applying the sign and multiplication rules.
2. Collect the like terms together.
3. Add or subtract the coefficients of like terms.
4. Write the final answer neatly, with the terms in a sensible order.

When you insert a bracket, the same idea runs backwards: pick out a common factor and group what is left inside. Your answer should always be checked — expand your bracket back out to confirm you get the original expression. If inserting and removing give you back what you started with, your algebra is correct.

<b>Worked example 1 — removing a bracket with a plus sign in front</b>

Simplify: 5 + (2x + 3)

Step 1: The bracket has + in front, so the signs inside stay. Remove it: 5 + 2x + 3.
Step 2: Collect the like terms. The numbers 5 and 3 are like terms: 5 + 3 = 8.
Step 3: Bring the 2x down: 2x + 8.

Answer: 2x + 8.

<b>Worked example 2 — removing a bracket with a minus sign in front</b>

Simplify: 4y − (2y + 1)

Step 1: The bracket has − in front, so every sign inside flips. Remove it: 4y − 2y − 1.
Step 2: Collect like terms. The y-terms match: 4y − 2y = 2y.
Step 3: Bring the −1 down: 2y − 1.

Answer: 2y − 1.

<b>Worked example 3 — multiplying a bracket by a number and a letter</b>

Simplify: 3(2a + 1) + 2(a − 4)

Step 1: Expand each bracket separately.
  3(2a + 1) = 3 × 2a + 3 × 1 = 6a + 3
  2(a − 4) = 2 × a − 2 × 4 = 2a − 8
Step 2: Combine the pieces: 6a + 3 + 2a − 8.
Step 3: Collect like terms. The a-terms: 6a + 2a = 8a. The numbers: 3 − 8 = −5.
Step 4: Write the answer: 8a − 5.

Answer: 8a − 5.

<b>Worked example 4 — inserting a bracket (factorising)</b>

Simplify by inserting a bracket: 6m + 9

Step 1: Find the biggest factor common to 6m and 9. The factors of 6m are 1, 2, 3, 6 and m; the factors of 9 are 1, 3, 9. The common factor is 3.
Step 2: Divide each term by 3: 6m ÷ 3 = 2m, and 9 ÷ 3 = 3.
Step 3: Write the common factor in front of the bracket with what is left inside: 3(2m + 3).

Check: expand 3(2m + 3) = 6m + 9. ✓

Answer: 3(2m + 3).

<b>Worked example 5 — inserting a bracket when the signs differ</b>

Simplify by inserting a bracket: 8p − 12

Step 1: The common factor of 8p and 12 is 4.
Step 2: Divide each term by 4: 8p ÷ 4 = 2p, and 12 ÷ 4 = 3. Because the signs differ, put the minus inside the bracket.
Step 3: Write: 4(2p − 3).

Check: expand 4(2p − 3) = 8p − 12. ✓

Answer: 4(2p − 3).

### From Words to Algebra: Solving Word Problems

A word problem is a story that hides a number question inside it. To solve it, you read the story, choose a letter for the unknown amount, and then use the algebra rules you already know to turn the sentences into one clean expression or equation.

Work through these three steps every time:
1. Choose a letter for the unknown. Write down what it stands for, for example "Let x be the number of mangoes."
2. Turn each sentence into a piece of algebra. Watch for the key words: "more than" means add, "less than" means subtract, "twice" and "double" mean multiply by 2, and "three times as many" means multiply by 3.
3. Join the pieces into one expression, simplify it (collect like terms), and if you can, solve it to find the number.

The important skill here is translating words into algebraic terms, then simplifying what you get, exactly like you have been doing with expressions.

Example 1: Tunde has t books. His sister has 5 more books than Tunde, and his friend has twice as many books as Tunde. Write a simplified expression for the total number of books.

Step 1: Let t be the number of books Tunde has.
Step 2: Turn each sentence into algebra.
- Sister has 5 more than Tunde: t + 5.
- Friend has twice as many as Tunde: 2t.
Step 3: Add the pieces for the total: t + (t + 5) + 2t.
Simplify by collecting like terms: t + t + 2t = 4t, and keep the + 5.
Total = 4t + 5 books.

Example 2: A number is multiplied by 3 and then 4 is added, and the result is 22. Find the number.

Let the number be n.
"Multiplied by 3": 3n. "Then 4 is added": 3n + 4. "The result is 22": 3n + 4 = 22.
Subtract 4 from both sides: 3n = 18.
Divide both sides by 3: n = 6.
The number is 6. Check: 3 × 6 + 4 = 18 + 4 = 22. Correct.

4. 2x − 2

   The minus in front means −1 × (4x + 2) = −4x − 2. Bring down the front: 6x − 4x − 2 = 2x − 2.

**Working with Brackets in Algebra**

1. 3x + 9

   The bracket has + in front, so signs stay: 7 + 3x + 2. Collect the numbers 7 + 2 = 9, keep 3x. Answer: 3x + 9.

2. 3a − 4

   The bracket has − in front, so signs flip: 5a − 2a − 4. The a-terms: 5a − 2a = 3a. Bring −4 down. Answer: 3a − 4.

3. 10b − 6

   Expand: 2(3b + 1) = 6b + 2, and 4(b − 2) = 4b − 8. Combine: 6b + 2 + 4b − 8. b-terms: 6b + 4b = 10b. Numbers: 2 − 8 = −6. Answer: 10b − 6.

4. 4(3m + 2)

   Common factor of 12m and 8 is 4. Divide: 12m ÷ 4 = 3m, 8 ÷ 4 = 2. Write: 4(3m + 2). Check: 4 × 3m + 4 × 2 = 12m + 8. ✓

5. 5(2n − 3)

   Common factor of 10n and 15 is 5. Divide: 10n ÷ 5 = 2n, 15 ÷ 5 = 3. Signs differ, so minus inside: 5(2n − 3). Check: 5 × 2n − 5 × 3 = 10n − 15. ✓

**From Words to Algebra: Solving Word Problems**

1. Let a be the number of oranges Adaeze has. Friend has a − 3, brother has 4a. Total = a + (a − 3) + 4a = 6a − 3 oranges.

   Let a be Adaeze's oranges. Friend: a − 3. Brother: 4a. Total = a + (a − 3) + 4a. Collect like terms: a + a + 4a = 6a, keep − 3. So the total is 6a − 3 oranges.

2. n = 11. Check: 2 × 11 − 7 = 22 − 7 = 15.

   Let the number be n. Doubled: 2n. Subtract 7: 2n − 7. This equals 15, so 2n − 7 = 15. Add 7 to both sides: 2n = 22. Divide by 2: n = 11. Check: 2(11) − 7 = 22 − 7 = 15. Correct.

3. Length = w + 6. Perimeter = 2(w + 6) + 2w = 4w + 12 cm.

   Width = w. Length = w + 6. Perimeter is the sum of all four sides: w + (w + 6) + w + (w + 6) = 4w + 12 cm.

4. m + (m + 25) + (m − 10) = 3m + 15 years.

   Chinedu: m. Father: m + 25. Sister: m − 10. Sum = m + (m + 25) + (m − 10). Collect like terms: m + m + m = 3m; and 25 − 10 = 15. So the sum is 3m + 15 years.
