## The revision pack

### Cylinders and Spheres: Getting to Know Their Shapes

Two shapes you see every day are the cylinder and the sphere. A tin of baked beans or a toilet roll is a cylinder; a football or an orange is a sphere. They are three-dimensional shapes, so they take up space, and each has its own special set of properties. Let us look at each one carefully.

<strong>The cylinder</strong>

Picture a tin of baked beans. A cylinder has two flat circular faces — one at the top and one at the bottom. These two circles are equal in size and lie parallel to each other. Between them runs a curved surface that joins the two circles, like the curved side of the tin. So a cylinder has:
<ul>
<li>two circular faces (the top and the bottom), equal and parallel;</li>
<li>one curved surface (the side);</li>
<li>no vertices (no sharp corner points);</li>
<li>two circular edges (the rims where each circular face meets the curved side).</li>
</ul>

If you count all the surfaces, a cylinder has 2 flat circular faces and 1 curved surface — 3 surfaces in all.

<strong>The sphere</strong>

Now picture a football. A sphere is perfectly round — every point on its surface is the same distance from its centre. A sphere has no flat faces, no edges and no corners. It is just one single curved surface that curves all the way around, with no starting or ending point.

<strong>Worked example</strong>

Is this statement true or false? <em>“A cylinder has two circular faces and one curved surface.”</em>

<em>Answer:</em> True. The two circular faces are the top and bottom of the cylinder, and the curved surface is the side that joins them. The statement describes the cylinder correctly.

<strong>Worked example</strong>

A ball has no flat faces, no edges and no vertices. Name the three-dimensional shape it is, and say how many surfaces it has.

<em>Answer:</em> The ball is a sphere. It has just one surface — a single curved surface that wraps all the way around it.

**Practice**

1. A solid cylinder has two circular faces (the top and the bottom) and one curved surface. How many faces does a cylinder have altogether?

2. How many circular faces does a solid cylinder have?

3. A sphere has no flat faces, no edges and no vertices. What is the name of its only surface?

### Finding the Volume of Cubes, Cuboids and Prisms

Volume tells you how much space a solid shape takes up. You measure it in cubic units, such as cubic centimetres (cm³) or cubic metres (m³). To find the volume of a cube or a cuboid, you multiply its length, its width and its height together.

Think of a cuboid as a stack of cubes. A cuboid that is 4 cm long, 2 cm wide and 3 cm high can be pictured as 3 layers, each layer holding 4 × 2 = 8 cubes. That gives 3 × 8 = 24 cubes, so its volume is 24 cm³. What you have done is simply multiply the three dimensions:

<center>Volume of a cuboid = length × width × height</center>

A cube is just a cuboid whose length, width and height are all the same size. So if a cube has edges of length <em>s</em>, its volume is:

<center>Volume of a cube = s × s × s = s³</center>

A prism is a solid with two identical, parallel faces (its bases) joined by flat rectangular sides. The volume of any prism is found by multiplying the area of one base by the height of the prism:

<center>Volume of a prism = area of base × height</center>

A cuboid is itself a prism whose base is a rectangle, so its base area is length × width, and multiplying by the height gives exactly the same formula as before.

<strong>Worked example 1: the volume of a cuboid</strong>

Find the volume of a cuboid that is 8 cm long, 4 cm wide and 3 cm high.

Use Volume = length × width × height.

Volume = 8 × 4 × 3 = 32 × 3 = 96 cm³.

So the cuboid takes up 96 cm³ of space.

<strong>Worked example 2: the volume of a cube</strong>

Find the volume of a cube whose edges are each 4 cm long.

Use Volume = s³ = s × s × s.

Volume = 4 × 4 × 4 = 16 × 4 = 64 cm³.

So the cube takes up 64 cm³ of space.

<strong>Worked example 3: the volume of a prism</strong>

A triangular prism has a base of area 12 cm² and a height of 5 cm. Find its volume.

Use Volume = area of base × height.

Volume = 12 × 5 = 60 cm³.

So the prism takes up 60 cm³ of space.

**Practice**

1. Find the volume of a cuboid that is 8 cm long, 4 cm wide and 3 cm high.

2. Find the volume of a cuboid with dimensions 5 m by 2 m by 3 m.

3. What is the volume of a cube of edges 4 cm × 4 cm × 4 cm?

4. A rectangular tank is 6 m long and 2 m wide and holds 36 m³ of water when full. How deep is the water in the tank?
