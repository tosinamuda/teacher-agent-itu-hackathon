## The revision pack

### Cylinders and Spheres: Their Properties

You already know the properties of cubes, cuboids, pyramids and cones. Now let's look at two more solid shapes: the cylinder and the sphere. These are three-dimensional figures, and each has its own set of properties.

<strong>A cylinder</strong> is a solid shape with two flat circular faces and one curved surface joining them. Picture a tin of milk or a roll of paper towel. Its properties are:
<ul>
<li>It has two circular faces, one at the top and one at the bottom.</li>
<li>The two circular faces are equal in size — they are identical circles.</li>
<li>It has one curved surface that wraps around and joins the two circles.</li>
<li>It has two edges — each edge is the circle where a circular face meets the curved surface.</li>
<li>It has no vertices (corners).</li>
</ul>
So when you count the faces of a cylinder, you get two flat circular faces and one curved face. Altogether a cylinder has three faces: two flat circles and one curved surface.

Before we go further, see if you can name the properties of a sphere yourself. A <strong>sphere</strong> is a perfectly round solid shape — think of a ball, a football, or an orange. Write down what you think its properties are, then read on to check.

A sphere's properties are:
<ul>
<li>It has <strong>no</strong> faces.</li>
<li>It has <strong>no</strong> edges.</li>
<li>It has <strong>no</strong> vertices.</li>
<li>It has one curved surface that covers the whole shape.</li>
<li>Every point on its surface is the same distance from its centre.</li>
</ul>
Unlike the cylinder, which has flat circular faces, the sphere is curved all over — that is why it has no flat faces, no edges and no corners.

Let's compare the two shapes side by side.

<strong>Question:</strong> A solid cylinder has how many faces?

<strong>Solution:</strong> A cylinder has two flat circular faces (one at the top, one at the bottom) and one curved surface that joins them. So altogether it has <strong>three</strong> faces: 2 circular faces + 1 curved face = 3 faces.

<strong>Question:</strong> How many circular faces does a solid cylinder have?

<strong>Solution:</strong> The two flat faces are the circular ones. So a solid cylinder has <strong>two</strong> circular faces.

<strong>Question:</strong> How many faces does a sphere have?

<strong>Solution:</strong> A sphere is curved all over, so it has no flat faces at all — it has <strong>zero</strong> faces (just one curved surface covering the whole shape).

**Practice**

1. A solid cylinder has how many faces altogether?

2. How many circular faces does a solid cylinder have?

3. State the number of faces, edges and vertices of a sphere.

4. A cylinder has some vertices. How many?

### Finding Volume: Cubes, Cuboids and Prisms

Volume is the amount of space a solid takes up. For a cube or a cuboid, the volume is found by multiplying the three dimensions together: length × width × height.

A **cuboid** is a box with rectangular faces. Its volume is:

Volume = length × width × height

A **cube** is a special cuboid where all three edges are the same length, so its volume is simply:

Volume = side × side × side

You can think of it this way: the bottom face of a cuboid has area length × width, and the height tells you how many of those layers of unit cubes stack up. So the volume is just the area of the base multiplied by the height. This same idea works for any prism: a prism has a shape at the front and back that is the same all the way through, so

Volume of a prism = area of base × height

For a cuboid the base is a rectangle, so area of base = length × width, which gives length × width × height.

**Example 1 — cuboid.** Find the volume of a cuboid 8 cm long, 4 cm wide and 3 cm high.

Volume = length × width × height = 8 × 4 × 3 = 96

So the volume is 96 cm³.

**Example 2 — cube.** Find the volume of a cube with edges 4 cm.

Volume = side × side × side = 4 × 4 × 4 = 64

So the volume is 64 cm³.

**Example 3 — prism.** A prism has a triangular base. The base has area 12 cm² and the prism is 5 cm long. Find its volume.

Volume = area of base × height = 12 × 5 = 60

So the volume is 60 cm³.

**Practice**

1. Calculate the volume of a cuboid 8 cm long, 4 cm wide and 3 cm high.

2. Calculate the volume of a cuboid with dimensions 5 m by 2 m by 3 m.

3. What is the volume of a cube of 4 cm × 4 cm × 4 cm?

4. A rectangular tank is 6 m long and 2 m wide, and it holds 36 m³ of water. How deep is the water in the tank?
