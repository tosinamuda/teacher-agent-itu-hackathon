### Cubes and Cuboids: Faces, Edges and Vertices

Around you are solid shapes that take up space. A box, a dice, a brick and a matchbox are all three-dimensional shapes, which means they have length, width and height. In this section you will look closely at two of them: the cube and the cuboid. To describe either one, you need three key ideas.

• A <b>face</b> is one of the flat surfaces of the shape.
• An <b>edge</b> is where two faces meet — the straight line between them.
• A <b>vertex</b> (plural: vertices) is a corner, where edges meet.

Let us see how these appear on a cube. A cube is the shape of a dice. Every one of its faces is a square, and all its faces are the same size. Count them: a cube has <b>6 faces</b>. Where the faces meet, there are straight edges, and a cube has <b>12 edges</b> — all of them the same length. Where the edges meet, there are corners, and a cube has <b>8 vertices</b>.

Now picture a cuboid, the shape of a brick or a box of cereal. A cuboid also has flat faces, but they are rectangles, and not all of them are the same size: the front and back are identical, the two sides are identical, and the top and bottom are identical. Like a cube, a cuboid has <b>6 faces</b>, <b>12 edges</b> and <b>8 vertices</b>. The difference is that the edges of a cuboid are not all equal — there are three different lengths (length, width and height), and each length is repeated four times.

So a cube and a cuboid are actually close cousins: both have 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices. What separates them is that every face of a cube is an equal square, while a cuboid has rectangular faces that may differ in size.

<b>Worked example 1</b><br>A dice is a cube. State the number of faces, edges and vertices on it.<br><br><b>Solution</b><br>A cube has 6 square faces. It has 12 edges, all of equal length. It has 8 vertices (corners). So the dice has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.

<b>Worked example 2</b><br>A cereal box is a cuboid. It has length 30 cm, width 10 cm and height 20 cm.<br>(a) How many faces does it have, and what shapes are they?<br>(b) How many edges of length 30 cm does it have?<br><br><b>Solution</b><br>(a) A cuboid has 6 faces, and they are rectangles.<br>(b) The length 30 cm appears four times, on the four edges that run along the length of the box. So there are 4 edges of length 30 cm.

**Practice**

1. Write down the number of faces, edges and vertices of a cube.

2. A brick is a cuboid. (a) How many faces does it have and what shape is each face? (b) A cuboid has edges of three different lengths — length, width and height. How many edges of each length does it have?

3. A cube and a cuboid both have the same number of faces, edges and vertices. State these three numbers, and explain the one way in which a cube is different from a cuboid.

### Pyramids and Cones: Faces, Edges and Vertices

A pyramid is a three-dimensional shape with one flat base and triangular side faces that all meet at a single point called the apex. The name of a pyramid tells you the shape of its base. For example, a pyramid with a triangular base is a triangular pyramid, and one with a square base is a square pyramid.

Every pyramid is described by three properties:
- Faces: the flat surfaces. A square pyramid has a square base plus four triangular side faces, so 5 faces in total.
- Vertices: the corners where faces meet. A square pyramid has 5 vertices — the 4 corners of the base and the apex.
- Edges: the straight lines where two faces meet. A square pyramid has 4 edges around the base and 4 edges slanting up to the apex, so 8 edges in total.

No matter what shape the base is, a pyramid always has just one apex, and its side faces are always triangles.

A cone is like a pyramid whose base is a circle instead of a polygon. A cone has:
- Faces: a cone has one flat circular base and one curved surface. The curved surface is not a flat face, so a cone is described as having 1 face.
- Vertices: a cone has one vertex, the pointed apex at the top.
- Edges: a cone has one edge, the circle where the curved surface meets the flat base.

The two key things to remember about a cone are that its base is a circle and its one curved surface slides up to a single point. Because the base is curved, a cone has only one edge (the circle), unlike a pyramid which has several straight edges.

Example: Count the faces, edges and vertices of a triangular pyramid.

Step 1 — Identify the base. The base is a triangle, so the pyramid has 1 triangular base.
Step 2 — Count the side faces. There is one triangular side face for each side of the base, so 3 side faces.
Step 3 — Add the faces together. 1 base + 3 side faces = 4 faces in total.
Step 4 — Count the vertices. The base triangle has 3 corners, and there is 1 apex on top, so 3 + 1 = 4 vertices.
Step 5 — Count the edges. The base has 3 edges and each corner of the base connects to the apex, giving 3 more edges. So 3 + 3 = 6 edges.

So a triangular pyramid has 4 faces, 4 vertices and 6 edges.

Example: Describe the faces, edges and vertices of a cone.

Step 1 — Look at the base. The base of a cone is a circle, so the cone has 1 flat face.
Step 2 — Look at the rest of the shape. The curved surface wraps around from the base up to the point, so that is 1 more surface.
Step 3 — Count the edges. The only edge is the circle where the curved surface meets the base, so the cone has 1 edge.
Step 4 — Count the vertices. The cone has one point at the top, so it has 1 vertex.

So a cone has 1 face, 1 edge and 1 vertex.

**Practice**

1. A pyramid has a square base. How many faces, vertices and edges does it have in total? Write your answer as three numbers, e.g. "x faces, y vertices, z edges".

2. Explain in one sentence what shape the side faces of any pyramid are, and what shape the base of any cone is.

3. A cone is sometimes said to have "one face, one edge and one vertex." In your own words, identify each of these three parts of a cone.

**Cubes and Cuboids: Faces, Edges and Vertices**

1. A cube has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.

2. (a) 6 faces, each a rectangle. (b) 4 edges of each length, so 4 length-edges, 4 width-edges and 4 height-edges.

   A cuboid has 6 faces, all rectangles. Its edges come in three lengths (length, width, height). There are 12 edges in total, so 12 ÷ 3 = 4 edges of each length.

3. Both have 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices. The difference is that every face of a cube is an equal square, while the faces of a cuboid are rectangles that may differ in size (and the edges of a cube are all equal, while a cuboid has three different edge lengths).

   Count on the shapes: each has 6 flat faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices. In a cube all six faces are identical squares and all edges are equal. In a cuboid the faces are rectangles and not all edges are equal — there are three lengths, each repeated four times.

**Pyramids and Cones: Faces, Edges and Vertices**

1. 5 faces, 5 vertices, 8 edges

   A square pyramid has 1 square base and 4 triangular side faces, so 1 + 4 = 5 faces. It has 4 base corners plus 1 apex, so 4 + 1 = 5 vertices. It has 4 edges around the base and 4 slanting edges to the apex, so 4 + 4 = 8 edges.

2. The side faces of any pyramid are triangles, and the base of any cone is a circle.

3. A cone has 1 face, 1 edge and 1 vertex.

   The one face is the flat circular base (the curved surface is not counted as a flat face). The one edge is the circle where the curved surface meets the base. The one vertex is the single pointed apex at the top of the cone.
