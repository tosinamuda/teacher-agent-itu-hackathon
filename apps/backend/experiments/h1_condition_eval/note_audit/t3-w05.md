#### Properties of a Sphere

A sphere drawn as a circle with an ellipse across its middle, to show it is round in every
direction.

[drawing attached to this section as unlabelled]

1. It is a solid shape.
2. It has one face, and it is curved.

4. It has no vertex.

#### 13.6 Volume of cubes and cuboids

Volume is the amount of the space that a 3D object takes up. Volume is a way of measuring the size of a whole 3D object. It is measured in cubic units.

When we measure length, we measure one dimension. The length of the line below is $1 \text{ cm}$. We say the length of the line is "1 centimetre".

**fig-01.** A single line segment, labelled 1 cm — one measured dimension.

[drawing attached to this section as fig-01]



When we measure area, we measure two dimensions. The square below has a length of 1 cm and a breadth (or width) of 1 cm. The area of the square is $1 \text{ cm}^{2}$. We say the area is "1 square centimetre".



**fig-02.** A square of side 1 cm, labelled on the bottom and right — two measured dimensions, giving an area of $1\,\text{cm}^2$.

[drawing attached to this section as fig-02]



When we measure volume. we measure three dimensions. The cube below has a length of 1 cm, a breadth of 1 cm and a height of 1 cm. The volume of the cube is $1 \text{ cm}^{3}$. We say the volume is "1 cubic centimetre".

**fig-03.** A cube of side 1 cm drawn in oblique projection with the hidden back edges dashed, labelled on length, breadth and height — three measured dimensions, giving a volume of $1\,\text{cm}^3$.

[drawing attached to this section as fig-03]



When we calculate the volume of a container, we work out how many cubes we can pack into the container.

We start by finding out how many cubes will cover the base of the container.

In the container below, it is possible to pack 6 cubes across the length of the container and 4 cubes across the breadth of the container.



**fig-04.** An open container with a single layer of unit cubes covering its base: 6 cubes along the length and 4 across the breadth, so $6 \times 4 = 24$ cubes in the layer.

[drawing attached to this section as fig-04]



To find the number of cubes for one layer, we multiply the number of cubes along the length by the number of cubes along the breadth.

To fill the whole container, we pack more layers. In the container below it is possible to pack 8 layers.

This container is a prism, so each layer will have the same number of cubes. To find the total number of cubes we multiply the number of cubes in one layer by the number of layers.



**fig-05.** The same container twice. Left: one layer of 24 cubes on the base. Right: the container filled, 8 layers deep, so $24 \times 8 = 192$ cubes in total.

[drawing attached to this section as fig-05]



From this calculation, we can see that we calculate the volume of a prism by multiplying the area of the base of the prism by the height of the prism.

#### Worked example 13.9: Calculating the volume of a cube

Find the volume of the following cube.



**fig-06.** A cube with every visible edge tick-marked to show the edges are equal, the right-hand edge labelled 3 cm.

[drawing attached to this section as fig-06]



Step 1: A cube is a prism. Write down the formula to find the volume of a prism. $$\text{Volume} = \text{area of base} \times \text{height of the prism}$$

Step 2: Find the area of the base of the cube. The base of a cube is a square. 

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Area of square} & = {s}^{2} \\ & = {3}^{2} \\ & = \text{9 cm}^{2} \end{aligned}$$

Step 3: Multiply the area of the base by the height of the prism to find the volume. 

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Volume} & = \text{area of base}\times \text{height of prism} \\ & = 9\times 3 \\ & = \text{27}\text{ cm}^{3} \end{aligned}$$

Step 4: Write the final answer, and remember to give the correct units. The volume of the cube is $\text{27 cm}^{3}$.

#### Worked example 13.10: Calculating the volume of a cuboid

The 3D object shown below is a cuboid. The height of the cuboid is 12 cm, the length is 13 cm, and the breadth is 6 cm. Calculate the volume of the object.

The diagram is not necessarily drawn to scale.



**fig-07.** A cuboid standing on its base, the base shaded to pick it out. The edges are labelled 13, 6 and 12; the hidden back edges are dashed.

[drawing attached to this section as fig-07]



Step 1: A cuboid is a prism. Write down the formula to find the volume of a prism. $$\text{Volume} = \text{area of base} \times \text{height of the prism}$$

Step 2: Find the area of the base of the cuboid. The purple rectangle at the bottom of the prism is the base of the cuboid. The purple rectangle at the bottom is the same size as the rectangle on the top. 

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Area of rectangle} & = l \times b \\ & = 13 \times 6 \\ & = \text{78 cm}^{2} \end{aligned}$$

Step 3: Multiply the area of the base of the prism by the height of the prism to find the volume. 

$$\begin{aligned} \text{Volume} & = \text{area of base}\times \text{height of prism} \\ & = 78\times 12 \\ & = \text{936 cm}^{3} \end{aligned}$$

Step 4: Write the final answer and remember to show the correct units. The volume of the cuboid is 936 cm $^{3}$.

#### Exercise 13.13: Find the volume of cubes and cuboids

The picture below shows a cube. The length, width and height of the cube are 5 cm. Find the volume of the cube.

**fig-08.** A cube with each edge 5 cm.

[drawing attached to this section as fig-08]

The volume of any prism is: $V = \text{area of base} \times \text{height of prism}$. A cube is a prism with a square as its base. 

$$\begin{array}{ll} {V}_\text{cube} & {= \text{area of base} \times \text{height of cube}} \\ & {= s \times s \times s}\\ & {= 5 \times 5 \times 5}\\ \text{V}_{cube} & {= \text{125 cm}^{3}} \\ \end{array}$$

 Answer: The volume of the cube is 125 cm $^{3}$.
