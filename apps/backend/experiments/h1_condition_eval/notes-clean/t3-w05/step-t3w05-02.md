#### Properties of a cylinder

**fig-01.** A cylinder drawn upright: an ellipse for the top face, two vertical sides
falling from its ends, and a curve closing the base.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.55,line width=0.5pt]
  \draw (-1.2,2.6) arc (180:-180:1.2 and 0.35);
  \draw (-1.2,2.6) -- (-1.2,0);
  \draw (1.2,2.6) -- (1.2,0);
  \draw (-1.2,0) arc (180:360:1.2 and 0.35);
  \draw[dashed] (-1.2,0) arc (180:0:1.2 and 0.35);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

1. It has two circular faces and one curved face
2. It has no vertex
3. It has two curved edges
4. It has three faces altogether

#### Properties of sphere

**fig-02.** A sphere drawn as a circle with an ellipse across its middle to show the
curved surface.

\begin{center}
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.55,line width=0.5pt]
  \draw (0,0) circle (1.3);
  \draw (-1.3,0) arc (180:360:1.3 and 0.42);
  \draw[dashed] (-1.3,0) arc (180:0:1.3 and 0.42);
\end{tikzpicture}
\end{center}

1. A sphere is symmetrical from all direction
2. It has no edge and no vertex
3. It has one curved surface
