# Write the steps and their activities

## A core step's blocks

The blocks are what the step teaches, in order. Start with an explanation.

| The objective is | The step has |
| --- | --- |
| concept | one explanation or more, and no worked example. Practice is allowed. |
| procedure | an explanation, a worked example and practice. |
| representation | an explanation, a worked example and practice. |

- A concept step with a worked example is wrong. A procedure step with only an explanation is
  wrong.
- A worked example gives the problem, the working one step at a time, each step with a label
  that says what it does, and the final answer.
- A practice block gives the question and the answer expected. Hints are optional, in the order
  to give them.
- Where the teacher's source gives the teaching for an objective, keep it, in its order. Do not
  replace its method with one of your own.
- Prepare the class for the objective's check questions: the step teaches what they ask.

## The introduction's blocks

- The introduction may have explanation blocks, and no other kind. It may have none.

## Activities

Activities are what happens in the step, in the order it runs. Each says who does it: the
teacher or the students.

- Write the activities in turn, as they happen: "The teacher works 1/4 + 2/4 on the board",
  then "The students copy the working", then "The students work 2/5 + 1/5 on their own".
- For every student activity in a core step, say whether it sets the students work of their own:
  - true: questions to answer, a task to complete on their own.
  - false: taking part in the teaching, such as listening, copying, or answering the teacher.
- Never give a teacher activity a value for sets_work. Leave it out.

## Minutes

- Give every step its minutes: a whole number, 1 or more.
- Add the minutes of all the steps, the introduction and the evaluation included. The total is
  no more than the lesson's time. If it is more, take minutes off the steps until it fits.
