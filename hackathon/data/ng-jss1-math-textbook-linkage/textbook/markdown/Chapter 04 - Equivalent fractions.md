## 4.1 Types of fractions

A **fraction** is part of a whole. There are different ways to write fractions. One way is to write the fraction as one whole number on top of another whole number with a dividing line between, as $\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}$.

The bottom number is called the **denominator**. It represents all the parts that the whole is divided into. The top number is called the **numerator**. It represents only the parts of the whole we are dealing with. Fractions that are written like this are called **common fractions**.

> **fraction** A fraction is a part of a whole.
>
> **denominator** The denominator is the number written below the line in a fraction; it represents all the parts of a whole.
>
> **numerator** The numerator is the number written above the line in a fraction; it represents only the parts of a whole we are dealing with.
>
> **common fraction** A common fraction is a fraction that is written in the form $\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}$, where the numerator and denominator are both whole numbers.

For example, if you cut a cake into seven pieces, you can say that the whole cake is made up of $\frac{7}{7}$.

![](https://electricbookworks.github.io/siyavula-open-textbooks-images/items/images/web/ebw-jss1-04-001.png)

Suppose you eat two of the pieces.

![](https://electricbookworks.github.io/siyavula-open-textbooks-images/items/images/web/ebw-jss1-04-002.png)

- The fraction of the cake you ate is $\frac{2}{7}$. The denominator shows that the cake was cut into 7 pieces. The numerator shows that 2 pieces were eaten.
- The fraction of the cake that is left over is $\frac{5}{7}$. The denominator still shows that the cake was cut into 7 pieces. The numerator now shows that 5 pieces are left over.

The fractions $\frac{2}{7}$ and $\frac{5}{7}$ are **proper fractions**. A proper fraction is smaller than 1. Its numerator is smaller than its denominator.

**proper fraction** A proper fraction is a fraction that is smaller than 1, so the numerator is smaller than the denominator.

A **mixed number** is made up of a whole number and a proper fraction. For example, $2\frac{1}{2}$ apples mean you have two whole apples and one half of another apple.

![](https://electricbookworks.github.io/siyavula-open-textbooks-images/items/images/web/ebw-jss1-04-012.png)

**mixed number** A mixed number is a number that is made up of a whole number and a proper fraction.

A mixed number may be expressed as an **improper fraction**. An improper fraction is larger than 1. Its numerator is larger than its denominator. Suppose we cut the two whole apples shown above into halves as well. We will then have five halves.

![](https://electricbookworks.github.io/siyavula-open-textbooks-images/items/images/web/ebw-jss1-04-013.png)

These five halves may be written as $\frac{5}{2}$. The denominator tells us that there are 2 parts in one whole apple, so we are dealing with halves. The numerator tells us that we have 5 halves. Therefore, the mixed number $2\frac{1}{2}$ may be expressed as the improper fraction $\frac{5}{2}$.

**improper fraction** An improper fraction is a fraction that is larger than 1, so the numerator is larger than the denominator.


### Worked example 4.1: Expressing mixed numbers as improper fractions

> Write $5\frac{3}{4}$ as an improper fraction.

1.  Step 1: Use the denominator to see how many parts there are in one whole.

    - The denominator of the proper fraction in this mixed number is 4.
    - This means that each whole is divided into 4 parts. We are dealing with quarters.

2.  Step 2: Determine how may parts the whole number represents. Multiply the whole number by the denominator from Step 1.

    - The whole number in this mixed number is 5.
    - If there are 4 parts in one whole, there must be $5\times4=20$ parts in five wholes.

3.  Step 3: Add the additional parts that are given by the numerator of the proper fraction.

    - The numerator of the proper fraction is 3.
    - So, as well as the 20 parts given by the whole number, we have another 3 parts.
    - The total parts are $20+3=23$.

4.  Step 4: Write the improper fraction in the form $\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}$.

    - The numerator gives the total parts we have: 23
    - The denominator tells us how many parts there are in one whole: 4

    $$
    \therefore 5\frac{3}{4}=\frac{23}{4}
    $$



### Worked example 4.2: Expressing improper fractions as mixed numbers

> Write $\frac{26}{3}$ as a mixed number.

1.  Step 1: Use the denominator to see how many parts there are in one whole.

    - The denominator of this improper fraction is 3.
    - This means that each whole is divided into 3 parts. We are dealing with thirds.

2.  Step 2: Determine how many whole numbers can be formed from the total parts we have. To do this, divide the numerator by the denominator from Step 1.

    - The numerator gives the total parts we have: 26
    - If there are 3 parts in one whole, we can form 8 wholes from 26 parts: $8\times3=24$

    $$
     \begin{array}{r}
     8\\
     3\enclose{longdiv}{26}\\
     \underline{-24} \\
     2
     \end{array}
    $$

    The whole number part of the mixed number is: 8

3.  Step 3: Determine what fraction is left over after we formed the whole numbers.

    - You know that the denominator, which tells us how many parts there are in one whole, is 3.
    - To work out the numerator, use the parts that are left over after we formed the whole numbers. If you look at the long division, the remainder is 2.

    After we formed the whole numbers, the fraction $\frac{2}{3}$ is left over.

4.  Step 4: Write down the mixed number as a whole number followed by a proper fraction.

    $$
    \therefore \frac{26}{3}=8\frac{2}{3}
    $$

## 4.2 Equivalent fractions

Fractions that represent the same part of a whole in different ways are called **equivalent fractions**. Look at the three diagrams below. The fraction of the rectangle that is shaded is given next to each diagram.

![](https://electricbookworks.github.io/siyavula-open-textbooks-images/items/images/web/ebw-jss1-04-015.png)

All three fractions represent half of the rectangle. The fractions have different numerators and denominators, but they all represent the value $\frac{1}{2}$.

$$
\therefore\frac{1}{2}=\frac{2}{4}=\frac{4}{8}
$$

**equivalent fractions** Equivalent fractions are fractions that have different numerators and denominators, but they represent the same part of a whole, and they have the same value.

### Find equivalent fractions

We find equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing the numerator and the denominator by the same whole number.


### Worked example 4.3: Working out equivalent fractions

> Find the missing numbers in these equivalent fractions:

$$
\frac{3}{4}=\frac{\square}{16}=\frac{6}{\square}=\frac{15}{\square}
$$

1.  Step 1: Start with the first pair. Identify what is given, which in this case is the two denominators. Was the first denominator multiplied or divided to get the second denominator, and by what whole number?

    $$
    \frac{3}{4}=\frac{\square}{16}
    $$

    The denominator was multiplied by 4 to get 16:

    $$
    \frac{3\phantom{000}}{4\times 4}=\frac{\square}{16}
    $$

2.  Step 2: Repeat the action identified in Step 1 for the remaining part of the fraction.

    The numerator must also be multiplied by 4:

    $$
    \frac{3\times 4}{4\times 4}=\frac{12}{16}
    $$

3.  Step 3: Repeat Steps 1 and 2 to find the next missing number.

    $$
    \frac{3}{4}=\frac{12}{16}=\frac{6}{\square}
    $$

    The numerator was divided by 2:

    $$
    \frac{3}{4}=\frac{12\div2}{16\phantom{000}}=\frac{6}{\square}
    $$

    The denominator must also be divided by 2:

    $$
    \frac{3}{4}=\frac{12\div2}{16\div 2}=\frac{6}{8}
    $$

4.  Step 4: Repeat Steps 1 and 2 for the last fraction. If it is not possible to perform Step 1 with the fraction to the left of the missing number, use any other fraction that is complete.

    $$
    \frac{3}{4}=\frac{12}{16}=\frac{6}{8}=\frac{15}{\square}
    $$

    It is not possible to multiply the numerator 6 or the numerator 12 by a whole number and then get 15 as an answer. You have to use the fraction $\frac{3}{4}$.

    The numerator was multiplied by 5:

    $$
    \frac{3}{4}=\frac{3\times5}{4\phantom{000}}=\frac{15}{\square}
    $$

    The denominator must also be multiplied by 5:

    $$
    \frac{3}{4}=\frac{3\times5}{4\times5}=\frac{15}{20}
    $$

5.  Step 5: Write down the full answer.

$$
\frac{3}{4}=\frac{12}{16}=\frac{6}{8}=\frac{15}{20}
$$


Improper fractions can also have equivalent fractions. Earlier on in this chapter, we showed that $2\frac{1}{2}$ apples is equal to $\frac{5}{2}$ apples. The improper fraction $\frac{5}{2}$ tells us that we are dealing with apple halves and that we have 5 apple halves.

![](https://electricbookworks.github.io/siyavula-open-textbooks-images/items/images/web/ebw-jss1-04-013.png)

Suppose we cut each apple half into two pieces. We will then have ten apple quarters.

![](https://electricbookworks.github.io/siyavula-open-textbooks-images/items/images/web/ebw-jss1-04-016.png)

This may be expressed as $\frac{10}{4}$. The denominator tells us that there are 4 parts in one whole apple, so we are dealing with quarters. The numerator tells us that we have 10 quarters. Therefore, the fractions $\frac{5}{2}$ and $\frac{10}{4}$ are equivalent fractions. In this case, the numerator and denominator were multiplied by 2:

$$
\frac{5\times2}{2\times2}=\frac{10}{4}
$$

### Simplify equivalent fractions

A fraction is in its **simplest form** when the numerator and the denominator have no common factor. For example, $\frac{1}{2}$ is the simplest form of the fractions $\frac{2}{4}$ and $\frac{4}{8}$. These fractions are not in their simplest form, because in $\frac{2}{4}$, 2 and 4 have 2 as a common factor, and in $\frac{4}{8}$, 4 and 8 have 2 and 4 as common factors.

**simplest form** A fraction is in its simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor.


### Worked example 4.4: Finding the simplest form of a fraction

> Find the simplest form of the fraction $\frac{40}{60}$.

1.  **Method 1 (Prime numbers)**

    **1.** Find the smallest prime number that is a factor of both the numerator and the denominator. In this case the factor is 2.\
    **2.** Divide both the numerator and denominator by this prime number. Write down the equivalent fraction.\
    **3.** Repeat until the prime number can no longer divide into both the numerator and the denominator.\
    **4.** Move on to the next prime number that is a factor of both the numerator and the denominator. In this case the factor is 5. Divide both the numerator and the denominator by 5.\
    **5.** Repeat this process until the numerator and the denominator have no more common factors.

    $$
    \frac{40\div2}{60\div2}=\frac{20}{30}
    $$
    $$
    \frac{20\div2}{30\div2}=\frac{10}{15}
    $$
    $$
    \frac{10\div5}{15\div5}=\frac{2}{3}
    $$

    The simplest form of $\frac{40}{60}$ is $\frac{2}{3}$.

2.  **Method 2 (HCF)**

    **1.** Express both the numerator and the denominator as a product of their prime numbers.\
    **2.** Find the HCF of the numerator and the denominator.\
    **3.** Divide the numerator and the denominator by their HCF.

    $$
    \begin{array}{r | r}
     2 & 40 \\
     \hline
     2 & 20 \\
     \hline
     2 & 10 \\
     \hline
     5 & 5 \\
     \hline
     & 1
     \end{array}
    $$
$$
40=2^3\times5
$$
$$
\begin{array}{r | r}
     2 & 60 \\
     \hline
     2 & 30 \\
     \hline
     3 & 15 \\
     \hline
     5 & 5 \\
     \hline
     & 1
     \end{array}
$$
$$
60=2^2\times3\times5
$$
$$
\therefore \text{HCF}=2^2\times5=2\times2\times5=20
$$
$$
\frac{40\div20}{60\div20}=\frac{2}{3}
$$

    The simplest form of $\frac{40}{60}$ is $\frac{2}{3}$.

    If necessary, go back to Chapter 2 to see how to find the highest common factor (HCF) of two or more numbers.

3.  **Method 3 (Inspection)**

    **1.** Find any common factor of the numerator and the denominator.\
    **2.** Divide both the numerator and denominator by this factor. Write down the equivalent fraction.\
    **3.** Repeat this process until the numerator and the denominator have no more common factors.

    $$
    \frac{40\div10}{60\div10}=\frac{4}{6}
    $$
    $$
    \frac{4\div2}{6\div2}=\frac{2}{3}
    $$

    The simplest form of $\frac{40}{60}$ is $\frac{2}{3}$.

### Identify equivalent fractions

Fractions that are equivalent have the same simplest form. Look at the two diagrams below. The fraction of the rectangle that is shaded is given next to each diagram.

![](https://electricbookworks.github.io/siyavula-open-textbooks-images/items/images/web/ebw-jss1-04-017.png)

It is easy to see that the fractions $\frac{6}{8}$ and $\frac{4}{8}$ are not equivalent. They do not represent the same part of the whole rectangle. We can check this by finding the simplest form of each fraction.

$$
\frac{6\div2}{8\div2}=\frac{3}{4}
$$
$$
\frac{4\div4}{8\div4}=\frac{1}{2}
$$
$$
\frac{3}{4}\neq\frac{1}{2}
$$

This shows that the fractions $\frac{6}{8}$ and $\frac{4}{8}$ do not have the same value. They are therefore not equivalent.

Remember that equivalent fractions have the same simplest form.

## 4.3 Comparing fractions

It may be difficult to compare fractions that do not have the same denominator. Look at the two diagrams below. The fraction of the circle that is shaded is given below each diagram.

![](https://electricbookworks.github.io/siyavula-open-textbooks-images/items/images/web/ebw-jss1-04-018.png)

Which fraction is larger, $\frac{2}{3}$ or $\frac{3}{4}$? It is not so easy to decide this just by looking at the diagrams or the fractions themselves. However, if we express both fractions with the same denominator, it becomes easier to compare them.

$$
\frac{2\times4}{3\times4}=\frac{8}{12}
$$
$$
\frac{3\times3}{4\times3}=\frac{9}{12}
$$

![](https://electricbookworks.github.io/siyavula-open-textbooks-images/items/images/web/ebw-jss1-04-019.png)

Now it is clear that $\frac{3}{4}$ is the larger fraction. We may write this in two ways:

- \\frac{2}{3}\<\\frac{3}{4} , which means $\frac{2}{3}$ is smaller than $\frac{3}{4}$

- $\frac{3}{4}&gt;\frac{2}{3}$, which means $\frac{3}{4}$ is larger than $\frac{2}{3}$

To be able to compare fractions, we first need to find the **lowest common denominator (LCD)** for the fractions. This is the lowest common multiple (LCM) of the denominators. Then we need to express the two fractions as equivalent fractions using this same denominator.

**lowest common denominator (LCD)** The LCD is the smallest whole number into which the denominators of two or more fractions can divide without a remainder.

If necessary, go back to Chapter 2 to see how to find the LCM of two or more numbers.

If we express more than two fractions using the same denominator, this allows us to order the fractions in **ascending order** or **descending order**. Fractions that are arranged in ascending order start with the smallest fraction, then have the next smallest, and so on, and the largest fraction is last. For example $\frac{1}{7};\,\frac{3}{7};\,\frac{6}{7}$. Fractions that are arranged in descending order start with the largest fraction, then have the next largest, and so on, and the smallest fraction is last. For example $\frac{5}{7};\,\frac{4}{7};\,\frac{2}{7}$.

> **ascending order** When numbers of any kind are arranged in ascending order, they are arranged from the smallest to the largest.
>
> **descending order** When numbers of any kind are arranged in descending order, they are arranged from the largest to the smallest.


### Worked example 4.5: Ordering fractions

> Arrange the following fractions in ascending order:

$$
\frac{5}{6};\,\frac{7}{8};\,\frac{3}{4}
$$

1.  Step 1: Before we can compare fractions, we need to give equivalent fractions using the same denominator. To do this, first find the LCD of the fractions.

    The denominators expressed as products of their prime factors are:\
    $6=2\times3$\
    $8=2^3$\
    $4=2^2$

    $$
    \therefore \text{LCD} = 2^3\times3=8\times3=24
    $$

2.  Step 2: For each given fraction, find an equivalent fraction using the denominator calculated in Step 1.

    $$
    \frac{5}{6}=\frac{\square}{24}\, \Rightarrow\, \frac{5\times4}{6\times4}=\frac{20}{24}
    $$
    $$
    \frac{7}{8}=\frac{\square}{24}\, \Rightarrow\, \frac{7\times3}{8\times3}=\frac{21}{24}
    $$
    $$
    \frac{3}{4}=\frac{\square}{24}\, \Rightarrow\, \frac{3\times6}{4\times6}=\frac{18}{24}
    $$

3.  Step 3: Write the original fractions in the required order.

    Ascending order means we have to start with the smallest fraction and end with the largest fraction: $\frac{18}{24};\;\frac{20}{24};\;\frac{21}{24}$.

    The original fractions, arranged in ascending order, are:

    $$
    \frac{3}{4};\,\frac{5}{6};\,\frac{7}{8}
    $$

## 4.4 Decimal fractions

Instead of writing a fraction as a common fraction, we can also write it as a **decimal fraction**. We use **decimal notation** to write decimal fractions. In this notation, the numerator is written after a decimal point and the denominator is not written down. Before you can write a common fraction as a decimal fraction, the denominator always has to be a power of ten.

In the first section of this chapter, you learnt that a mixed number is a whole number and a proper fraction. Mixed numbers may also be written in decimal notation. The decimal point separates the whole numbers from the fractions. For example, 2.5 means that we have 2 wholes and a fraction of $\frac{5}{10}$.

> **decimal fraction** A decimal fraction is a fraction that is written in decimal notation, with the whole number before the decimal point and the fraction after it.
>
> **decimal notation** Decimal notation is a way of writing a fraction by putting the numerator after a decimal point. The denominator is not written down.

In decimal notation, the number of digits after the decimal point shows us which power of ten is the fraction\'s denominator. You learnt about these decimal fractions in Chapter 1:

$$
\begin{array}{|l|l|l|l|}
  \hline
    \textbf{Name}  & \textbf{Common fraction} & \textbf{Powers of ten} & \textbf{Decimal fraction}    \\
    \hline
     \text{One tenth} & \frac{1}{10} & \frac{1}{10}=10^{-1} & 0.1    \\
    \hline
    \text{One hundredth} & \frac{1}{100} & \frac{1}{10\times10}=10^{-2} & 0.01    \\
    \hline
    \text{One thousandth} & \frac{1}{1,000} & \frac{1}{10\times10\times10}=10^{-3} & 0.001    \\
    \hline
    \text{One ten thousandth} & \frac{1}{10,000} & \frac{1}{10\times10\times10\times10}=10^{-4} & 0.0001   \\
    \hline
     \end{array}
$$

You do not need to write zeros at the end of decimal fractions. For example, we write 0.5 rather than 0.50 or 0.500. This is because $\frac{5}{10}=\frac{50}{100}=\frac{500}{1,000}$.

### Converting decimal fractions to common fractions

The fraction 0.5 and the fraction $\frac{1}{2}$ represent exactly the same part of a whole, namely a half. The fractions $\frac{5}{10}$ and $\frac{1}{2}$ are equivalent fractions. A decimal fraction and a common fraction are just different ways of expressing the same value.

### Converting common fractions to decimal fractions

When a common fraction has a denominator that is a power of ten, it is easy to convert it to a decimal fraction. You dealt with the place values of decimal fractions in Chapter 1.

$$
\begin{array}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
  \hline
    \text{units} & \text{.} & \text{tenths} & \text{hundredths} & \text{thousandths} & \text{ten thousandths} \\
    (1) & \text{.} & \left( \frac{1}{10} \right) & \left( \frac{1}{100} \right) & \left( \frac{1}{1,000} \right) & \left( \frac{1}{10,000} \right) \\
    \text{u} & \text{.} & \text{t} & \text{h} & \text{th} & \text{tth} \\
    \hline
    \end{array}
$$

Consider the fraction $\frac{30}{1,000}$. If the denominator is 1,000, the corresponding decimal fraction must have 3 digits after the decimal point. We may write it in a place value table.

$$
\begin{array}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
  \hline
    (1) & \text{.} & \left( \frac{1}{10} \right) & \left( \frac{1}{100} \right) & \left( \frac{1}{1,000} \right) & \left( \frac{1}{10,000} \right) \\
    \text{u} & \text{.} & \text{t} & \text{h} & \text{th} & \text{tth} \\
    \hline
    0 & \text{.} & 0 & 3 & 0 &  \\
    \hline
    \end{array}
$$

The place value table shows that we have zero units, zero tenths, three hundredths and zero thousandths. Remember that we do not write zeros at the end of decimal fraction. This fraction is therefore written as 0.03.

Similarly, the common fraction $\frac{30}{100}$ may be expressed as 0.30, which is written as 0.3. The common fraction $\frac{30}{10}$ may be expressed as 3.0, which is written as 3.

## 4.5 Percentages

As well as writing a fraction as a common fraction or a decimal fraction, we may also express a fraction as a **percentage**. A percentage is a fraction in which the denominator is always 100. Only the numerator is written down, followed by a percentage symbol: %. The symbol shows that the number we wrote down gives the parts of a whole that is divided into 100.

**percentage** A percentage is a fraction in which the denominator is 100 and where only the numerator is written down, followed by a percentage symbol.

This figure is divided into 100 parts. There are 22 blocks that are shaded. The common fraction $\frac{22}{100}$ tells you what part is shaded. To express this as a percentage, we write only the numerator, followed by a percentage symbol: 22%.

![](https://electricbookworks.github.io/siyavula-open-textbooks-images/items/images/web/ebw-jss1-04-020.png)

If we shade all the blocks in the figure, the shaded fraction will be $\frac{100}{100}=1$. One full whole is therefore 100%.

### Converting percentages to common fractions

It is easy to convert percentages to common fractions. We simply use the form $\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}$. We know that the denominator is always 100, so we have $\frac{\text{numerator}}{100}$. The numerator is the number before the percentage symbol.

For example, $36\% = \frac{36}{100}$. The simplest form of this fraction is $\frac{36\div4}{100\div4}=\frac{9}{25}$.

## 4.7 Summary

- A common fraction is a fraction that is written in the form $\frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}}$, where the numerator and denominator are both whole numbers.
- A proper fraction is smaller than 1. The numerator is smaller than the denominator, for example $\frac{2}{7}$.
- A mixed number consists of a whole number and a proper fraction, for example $5\frac{1}{6}$.
- An improper fraction is larger than 1. The numerator is larger than the denominator, for example $\frac{9}{4}$.
- Equivalent fractions have different numerators and denominators, but they represent the same part of a whole and therefore the same value. For example, $\frac{1}{2}=\frac{2}{4}=\frac{4}{8}$.
- A fraction is in its simplest form when the numerator and denominator have no common factor.
- To be able to compare fractions, we must express them with the same denominators. The lowest common denominator (LCD) of two or more fractions is the smallest whole number into which all the denominators can divide without a remainder.
- A decimal fraction is a fraction expressed in decimal notation, where the numerator is written after a decimal point and the denominator is not written down. The denominator in a decimal fraction must be a power of ten.
- In decimal notation, the decimal point separates whole numbers from fractions.
- In decimal notation, the number of digits after the decimal point shows us which power of ten is the fraction\'s denominator. For example, if there are three digits after the decimal point, the denominator is $10\times10\times10=1,000$.
- A percentage is a fraction of which the denominator is 100 and where only the numerator is written down, followed by a percentage symbol.
