# Write worked examples and practice

## Worked examples

- Give at least two worked examples, from simple to harder, the second harder than the first.
- State the problem, then solve it line by line. Write each line of working in LaTeX
  between $ signs (plain text in a lesson that does not calculate), with a short commentary that says why it follows. Never give commentary
  without its working.
- The final answer follows from the last line of working.
- Work on what the objective names: a section on algebraic terms works on algebraic terms,
  not on bare numbers.

## Practice

- Give at least three practice questions, each asking something different: change the
  numbers, the form or the context, never only the numbers. Every question has its `answer`.
- Never write the answer, or a hint that gives it away, into the `question`. The learner
  attempts first, then checks.
- Never add an "Answers" heading or answer list of your own. Each answer stays with its
  question in `answer`.
- Give a `worked_solution` for every question that takes more than one step, so a learner
  who got it wrong can see where. Put the working there, never in the question.
- Practise what the worked examples taught, of the same kind, at the class's level. When the
  lesson taught a method, such as prime factorisation for the HCF, set questions that use it.
- Put a calculation's working in `worked_solution`. `answer` holds the answer: the number,
  the list or the statement the learner compares with.
- A question that needs a drawing uses one in `figures`. Never put TikZ source in a
  question or an answer.
